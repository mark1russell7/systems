# S3 · Outcomes under failure and load

S1 and S2 ask when a client learns of a fault. S3 asks what became of the work of the client. This file holds the plan of S3. The status of each item is in [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md#the-items-of-s3).

## The three questions

- **Q17, the writes.** Under each manifestation, which acknowledged writes are lost or duplicated, for each system and client configuration?
- **Q18, the storms.** Under load, which failures turn into retry storms, and does the system recover when the trigger stops?
- **Q19, the exhaustion.** At what load does each pool, port range, conntrack table and accept backlog saturate, and what does the client see?

S3 is done when each question has an answer that a checker confirmed. Kafka and etcd join the set of systems in S3.

## The rules of the plan

- **Each item is a measured question with a law.** The law comes before the dispatch, from a pinned source. The band does not change after a result.
- **The algebra predicts which outcomes are possible, and a checker tells which ones occurred.** A count of lost writes needs a law from the timers, and also a check of the history.
- **A count comes first.** An exact count, for example tries for each request, needs no load generator. Thus each question starts with a count.
- **The first honest result comes early.** The order below reuses the labs of S1 before it builds a lab.

## What S1 already records

S3 does not start from zero. Each group cell of S1 already records a history of operations:

- The client of a group writes a `sent` line for each operation. It then writes an `ack` line, or a `fail` line with its error.
- The harness reads the keys that the group kept after the run (`holds`).
- `writesOf` gives the writes that a primary accepted and the group did not keep (`lost`), and the writes with no answer (`unknown`).

These are the three outcomes of a Jepsen history: `ok`, `fail` and `info`. The difference of the acknowledged set and the final read is the set check of Jepsen. It needs no solver, thus no new language.

The results of 2026-10-17 report these counts, but no prediction judges them. The partition of DV-40 lost 1,041 writes in the median run. The freeze of 40 s lost 199. The first step of S3 is a law for such a count.

## The first cell: SYS-58

The question: how many accepted writes does a Valkey failover lose, and does `min-replicas-to-write` bound that count?

The partition of DV-40 is the worst case of T12, and `T12.defaults.1` has no law for it. Its client stays on the old primary, and the old primary accepts each write. The count grows with the hold of the partition and with the time to the conversion of the old primary. No document gives the second term.

`min-replicas-to-write 1` removes that term. The old primary refuses writes when no replica acknowledged in `min-replicas-max-lag` seconds. Thus the count must not depend on the hold.

### The terms

Each term comes from the source of Valkey 9.1.2:

- A replica sends `REPLCONF ACK` from `replicationCron`, each 1 s (`src/replication.c:5296`, `src/server.c:1664`).
- The primary stores `server.unixtime` as the time of the acknowledgment, in whole seconds (`src/replication.c:1462`).
- `refreshGoodReplicasCount` counts a replica as good while `lag <= min-replicas-max-lag` (`src/replication.c:4933-4935`).
- The primary refreshes that count at the end of `replicationCron`, each 1 s (`src/replication.c:5439`). It also refreshes it when a replica client goes (`src/replication.c:1334`).
- `processCommand` refuses a write with `NOREPLICAS` while the count is below the setting (`src/server.c:4550`).
- An EOF on a client socket frees that client after the current batch of events (`src/networking.c:3183`).

### The law of the partition

Let L be `min-replicas-max-lag`, 10 s here. Let t be the tick of 100 ms of the server (`hz` 10).

- The last acknowledgment reached the primary up to 1 s and a tick before the fault.
- The whole seconds put the first refresh that fails between L and L + 2 s after that acknowledgment, and a tick later at most. The stale time cache can move the low edge one tick.
- Thus the first refusal comes 8.8 s to 12.1 s after the fault. The client sees it up to one cycle later, and a cycle is 50 ms to 62 ms.
- The accepted writes in that window are the lost writes. Each cycle is the pause of 50 ms, up to 2 ms of round trip and up to 10 ms of the event loop.
- The low edge is 8.8 s at 62 ms for each write, less one. The high edge is 12.1 s at 50 ms for each write, plus two: one in flight at the fault, and one at the refusal.

This gives **140 to 244 lost writes**, for each hold longer than the failover. Two holds test that: 40 s and 80 s. The recorded control with no setting lost 1,041 at 40 s.

### The law of the freeze

A freeze of 40 s ends after the failover of 30 s to 33 s. Each replica closed its link to the frozen primary in the failover, and the kernel of that primary holds each FIN.

- At the thaw, the event loop reads the EOF of each replica. The refresh comes after that batch of events.
- Thus the primary accepts at most the one write that waited through the freeze. One more can have missed its replication at the freeze.
- This gives **0 to 2 lost writes**. The recorded control with no setting lost 199.
- The first refusal comes at the thaw, up to one cycle later.

### The cells

| Cell | Fault | Hold | Lost writes | First `NOREPLICAS` from the fault |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `T12.writes.1` | Partition from the group | 40 s | 140 to 244 | 8.8 s to 12.162 s |
| `T12.writes.2` | Partition from the group | 80 s | 140 to 244 | 8.8 s to 12.162 s |
| `T12.writes.3` | Freeze of the primary | 40 s | 0 to 2 | 40 s to 40.062 s |

Each cell has 4 counted runs in 2 environments. It uses the lab, the image and the records of T12, with one setting changed. A group has 16 containers and one cell operates at a time, thus the cells fit one runner of 4 CPUs and 16 GB.

The harness gets one new field: a band of lost writes in the prediction of a group. A measurement-only cell with that band gets a decision from the band.

### The result

All three cells hold with 4 counted runs each. The partitions lose 203 to 232 writes, at each hold, and the freeze loses 1 write in each run. [The roadmap](ROADMAP.md#sys-58--valkey-the-accepted-writes-that-a-failover-loses) has the table and the open term of the low edge.

## The COMMIT with no reply: SYS-61

The question: what is the outcome of a COMMIT in flight at the fault?

The plan said that a final read splits those commits into two sets, and that a synchronous standby bounds the lost set. The source of PostgreSQL 17.11 changes that law in two ways. A failover gives three sets, thus two final reads. A cancel removes the bound of a synchronous standby.

### The terms of the COMMIT

Each term comes from the tag REL_17_11. Each path is under `src/backend` unless it starts with `src/include` or `doc`.

- A simple query commits before it replies: `finish_xact_command` comes first (`tcop/postgres.c:1304`), then `EndCommand` (`tcop/postgres.c:1343`).
- The commit holds interrupts from its start to its end (`access/transam/xact.c:2302`, `:2460`).
- `RecordTransactionCommit` flushes the WAL first (`access/transam/xact.c:1491`). It marks the commit log next (`:1497`). It waits for the standby last (`:1546`).
- The WAL sender sends only WAL that is flushed (`replication/walsender.c:3249`). Thus an ack can go to the client before the standby has its WAL.
- With no name in `synchronous_standby_names`, the wait ends at once (`replication/syncrep.c:209`). The default is an empty text (`utils/misc/guc_tables.c:4572`).
- The default of `synchronous_commit` is `on` (`utils/misc/guc_tables.c:4926`). That level waits for the flush of the standby (`src/include/access/xact.h:70`).
- The standby replies after its flush (`replication/walreceiver.c:1070`, `:1110`). The reply ends the wait (`replication/walsender.c:2540`, `replication/syncrep.c:285`).

### The terms of the wait

- The wait has no timeout (`replication/syncrep.c:331`).
- `statement_timeout` does not bound it: its timer stops before the commit (`tcop/postgres.c:2802`).
- `wal_sender_timeout` stops the WAL sender and does not release the wait (`replication/walsender.c:2815`).
- A client that closes does not end the wait. Interrupts are held (`tcop/postgres.c:3275`), and the check of the socket is in `ProcessInterrupts` (`tcop/postgres.c:3321`).
- A cancel ends the wait with a WARNING: "canceling wait for synchronous replication due to user request". The commit then continues to a reply with success (`replication/syncrep.c:317` to `:325`).
- A stop of the backend ends the wait with a WARNING that says that the transaction "has already committed locally". It sends no reply (`replication/syncrep.c:300` to `:309`).
- The death of the postmaster ends the wait with no message (`replication/syncrep.c:338` to `:344`).
- Crash recovery finishes each commit that the wait held. The primary can then show a commit that the standby does not show (`doc/src/sgml/high-availability.sgml:1344`).

The manual says that data is lost only if the primary and the standby crash together (`doc/src/sgml/high-availability.sgml:1088`). The cancel path is a second way. T10.commit.5 tests it.

### The sets

A write with no reply is `info` in the history. Two final reads put it in one of three sets:

| Set | The new primary | The old primary, after its crash recovery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kept | Holds it | Holds it |
| Stranded | Does not hold it | Holds it |
| Absent | Does not hold it | Does not hold it |

Let K be the writers of the client, each with one write at a time. A writer stops at its first failure, and a write with no reply holds its writer. Thus **the count of writes with no reply is exactly K**. The time of the window at the fault does not enter this count. A writer sends one write in each cycle.

The run must promote the standby with the cut still in place, and with the old primary stopped. A lift before that gives the standby its missing WAL. The steps are:

1. The cut, and the hold.
2. A stop of each process of the primary.
3. The promotion, and the first read.
4. A restart of the old primary, and the second read.

### The law of the lost writes

A lost write is an ack that the new primary does not hold. K is 4, the pause of a writer I is 50 ms, and the hold H is 10 s. H is shorter than `wal_sender_timeout`, 60 s (`replication/walsender.c:127`).

- **Each peer cut, no synchronous standby.** Each writer can have one ack that the cut stops on its way to the standby. This gives 0 to K: **0 to 4**.
- **Each peer cut, a synchronous standby.** An ack follows the flush of the standby. This gives **0**.
- **The standby cut, no synchronous standby.** The client continues, and each write of the hold is lost. A cycle is I, and at most one commit more. This is the commit time C, 20 ms. No source gives C. The low edge is `K·H/(I + C)` less K. The high edge is `K·H/I` plus K. This gives **567 to 804**.
- **The standby cut, a synchronous standby.** Each writer stops in the wait, with its WAL flushed. This gives **0**.
- **The same, with two cancels.** Each cancel releases K writes with an ack and a WARNING. The writes of the second cancel are on no standby. The writes of the first can be on it, if their WAL crossed before the cut. This gives K to 2K: **4 to 8**, and exactly 8 warnings.

### The cells

Each cell has K writers, 4 counted runs and one environment for each run, because a run promotes the standby. The two rows that say "measured" have no law for the split: it depends on where the cut finds each write. A band for it has no justification, thus the cell reports that count and no band judges it.

| Cell | The cut | Synchronous standby | Lost writes | No reply | Kept | Absent | Warnings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `T10.commit.1` | Each peer | No | 0 to 4 | 4 | Measured | Measured | 0 |
| `T10.commit.2` | Each peer | Yes | 0 | 4 | Measured | Measured | 0 |
| `T10.commit.3` | The standby | No | 567 to 804 | 4 | 0 | Measured | 0 |
| `T10.commit.4` | The standby | Yes | 0 | 4 | Measured | 0 | 0 |
| `T10.commit.5` | The standby, two cancels | Yes | 4 to 8 | 4 | Measured | 0 | 8 |

Only a cancel gives a warning here: the injector stops the postmaster first, thus no backend gets the warning of a crash. In each cell, each ack is on the old primary: the flush comes before the reply.

The contrast of the two settings is in `T10.commit.3` and `T10.commit.4`, where the standby alone is cut. When each peer is cut, the client stops with each setting, and the contrast is at most 4 writes.

The harness gets a pair (`Cell.pair`): a standby next to the target of a cell. The client gets K writers with a sequence number in the text of each write, and a line for each notice of the server. The prediction gets bands of counts for the sets (`GroupPrediction.outcomes`).

`T10.defaults.18` stays open. It asks for a fault at a timed instant, and these cells do not need one: a hold finds each write of a writer.

## The first count: SYS-67

The question: how many tries does one request cost across hops? Law O08 says that the tries of each hop multiply. The count needs no load generator.

The deepest listener sees `A = t0 × t1 × … × tn` tries for one logical request. Here `ti` is the tries of caller i against the answer of the listener below it. An answer that a caller does not retry gives `ti = 1`, thus it stops the product above it.

Each term comes from a pinned source:

- The `RetryAgent` of undici 8.12.2 has `maxRetries` 5, seven methods and five statuses at its defaults (`lib/handler/retry-handler.js:125-133`).
- Each reply adds one to its counter (`:380`), and each socket error adds one (`:610`). It stops when the counter passes `maxRetries` (`:348`). Thus it sends 6 times.
- It does not retry a method or a status out of its lists (`:332`, `:338`). A close with no reply is in its list (`:144`).
- The `Transport` of Go 1.27.2 ends the round trip at each reply (`src/net/http/transport.go:727-737`). Thus a status costs 1 try.
- On a reused connection, a read failure sends a replayable request again (`:877-881`, `:2510`). A fresh connection does not (`:858`). Thus a GET that meets a close costs 2 tries.
- `retry` of `@mark1russell7/service` 0.6.5 counts the first try in `tries` (`packages/service/src/retrier.ts:121-125`).

The target is one process with a chain of listeners on loopback, each with a real retry client. Each listener writes a line for each arrival, thus the record shows each term. No hop needs a container, and the cell uses no fault. The count reads no network term and no timer, thus a loaded runner cannot move it.

| Cell | Client | Hops | Failure | Tries | Count at the target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `T14.count.1` | undici | None | 503 | 6 | 6 |
| `T14.count.2` | undici | undici | 503 | 6 × 6 | 36 |
| `T14.count.3` | undici, 1 ms | undici, 1 ms | 503 | 6 × 6 | 36 |
| `T14.count.4` | undici, 1 ms | `retry` with 3 tries, undici | 503 | 6 × 3 × 6 | 108 |
| `T14.count.5` | Go | undici | 503 | 1 × 6 | 6 |
| `T14.count.6` | undici, POST | undici | 503 | 1 × 1 | 1 |
| `T14.count.7` | undici | undici that answers 501 | 503 | 1 × 6 | 6 |
| `T14.count.8` | undici | undici | Close | 6 × 6 | 36 |
| `T14.count.9` | Go | None | Close | 2 | 2 |

Each cell has 4 counted runs in 2 environments. `T14.count.2` takes approximately 108.5 s for each run, and the other cells take seconds. The harness gets one new field: exact counts of the lines of the target, in the prediction of a measurement-only cell.

## The choice of a checker

Q17 needs no solver: a set difference and a count give its answers. A check of linearizability needs a solver, and a check of transactions needs Elle. [D-CHECKER](ROADMAP.md#d-checker--the-checker-of-linearizability-and-of-transactions) chose Porcupine for SYS-75, as a program that CI operates on each history. No package links it. Elle is a later choice, for a transactional system.

## The homes

Three parts of S3 have a home question:

- A load schedule has the planned home `mark1russell7/rigor`. SYS-68 waits for the open-loop schedule in that repository. Do not write a local schedule.
- A general checker of histories has the home `mark1russell7/rigor`, as an oracle in its test helpers. Until rigor has it, the checker stays in its own folder of this repository, with no import of the code of this repository. The set check of SYS-58 stays in `writesOf`, where it already is.
- The drivers of the load are lab code. They stay in `packages/harness/image`.
