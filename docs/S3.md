# S3 · Outcomes under failure and load

S1 and S2 ask when a client learns of a fault. S3 asks what became of the work of the client. This file holds the plan of S3. The status of each item is in [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md#the-items-of-s3).

## The three questions

- **Q17, the writes.** Under each manifestation, which acknowledged writes are lost or duplicated, for each system and client configuration?
- **Q18, the storms.** Under load, which failures turn into retry storms, and does the system recover when the trigger stops?
- **Q19, the exhaustion.** At what load does each pool, port range, conntrack table and accept backlog saturate, and what does the client see?

S3 is done when each question has an answer that a checker confirmed. Kafka and etcd join the set of systems in S3.

## The rules of the plan

- **Each item is a measured question with a law.** The law comes before the dispatch, from a pinned source. The band does not change after a result.
- **The algebra predicts which outcomes are possible, and a checker tells which ones occurred.** A count of lost writes needs a law from the timers, and also a check of the history.
- **A count comes first.** An exact count, for example tries for each request, needs no load generator. Thus each question starts with a count.
- **The first honest result comes early.** The order below reuses the labs of S1 before it builds a lab.

## What S1 already records

S3 does not start from zero. Each group cell of S1 already records a history of operations:

- The client of a group writes a `sent` line for each operation. It then writes an `ack` line, or a `fail` line with its error.
- The harness reads the keys that the group kept after the run (`holds`).
- `writesOf` gives the writes that a primary accepted and the group did not keep (`lost`), and the writes with no answer (`unknown`).

These are the three outcomes of a Jepsen history: `ok`, `fail` and `info`. The difference of the acknowledged set and the final read is the set check of Jepsen. It needs no solver, thus no new language.

The results of 2026-10-17 report these counts, but no prediction judges them. The partition of DV-40 lost 1,041 writes in the median run. The freeze of 40 s lost 199. The first step of S3 is a law for such a count.

## The first cell: SYS-58

The question: how many accepted writes does a Valkey failover lose, and does `min-replicas-to-write` bound that count?

The partition of DV-40 is the worst case of T12, and `T12.defaults.1` has no law for it. Its client stays on the old primary, and the old primary accepts each write. The count grows with the hold of the partition and with the time to the conversion of the old primary. No document gives the second term.

`min-replicas-to-write 1` removes that term. The old primary refuses writes when no replica acknowledged in `min-replicas-max-lag` seconds. Thus the count must not depend on the hold.

### The terms

Each term comes from the source of Valkey 9.1.2:

- A replica sends `REPLCONF ACK` from `replicationCron`, each 1 s (`src/replication.c:5296`, `src/server.c:1664`).
- The primary stores `server.unixtime` as the time of the acknowledgment, in whole seconds (`src/replication.c:1462`).
- `refreshGoodReplicasCount` counts a replica as good while `lag <= min-replicas-max-lag` (`src/replication.c:4933-4935`).
- The primary refreshes that count at the end of `replicationCron`, each 1 s (`src/replication.c:5439`). It also refreshes it when a replica client goes (`src/replication.c:1334`).
- `processCommand` refuses a write with `NOREPLICAS` while the count is below the setting (`src/server.c:4550`).
- An EOF on a client socket frees that client after the current batch of events (`src/networking.c:3183`).

### The law of the partition

Let L be `min-replicas-max-lag`, 10 s here. Let t be the tick of 100 ms of the server (`hz` 10).

- The last acknowledgment reached the primary up to 1 s and a tick before the fault.
- The whole seconds put the first refresh that fails between L and L + 2 s after that acknowledgment, and a tick later at most. The stale time cache can move the low edge one tick.
- Thus the first refusal comes 8.8 s to 12.1 s after the fault. The client sees it up to one cycle later, and a cycle is 50 ms to 62 ms.
- The accepted writes in that window are the lost writes. Each cycle is the pause of 50 ms, up to 2 ms of round trip and up to 10 ms of the event loop.
- The low edge is 8.8 s at 62 ms for each write, less one. The high edge is 12.1 s at 50 ms for each write, plus two: one in flight at the fault, and one at the refusal.

This gives **140 to 244 lost writes**, for each hold longer than the failover. Two holds test that: 40 s and 80 s. The recorded control with no setting lost 1,041 at 40 s.

### The law of the freeze

A freeze of 40 s ends after the failover of 30 s to 33 s. Each replica closed its link to the frozen primary in the failover, and the kernel of that primary holds each FIN.

- At the thaw, the event loop reads the EOF of each replica. The refresh comes after that batch of events.
- Thus the primary accepts at most the one write that waited through the freeze. One more can have missed its replication at the freeze.
- This gives **0 to 2 lost writes**. The recorded control with no setting lost 199.
- The first refusal comes at the thaw, up to one cycle later.

### The cells

| Cell | Fault | Hold | Lost writes | First `NOREPLICAS` from the fault |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `T12.writes.1` | Partition from the group | 40 s | 140 to 244 | 8.8 s to 12.162 s |
| `T12.writes.2` | Partition from the group | 80 s | 140 to 244 | 8.8 s to 12.162 s |
| `T12.writes.3` | Freeze of the primary | 40 s | 0 to 2 | 40 s to 40.062 s |

Each cell has 4 counted runs in 2 environments. It uses the lab, the image and the records of T12, with one setting changed. A group has 16 containers and one cell operates at a time, thus the cells fit one runner of 4 CPUs and 16 GB.

The harness gets one new field: a band of lost writes in the prediction of a group. A measurement-only cell with that band gets a decision from the band.

### The result

All three cells hold with 4 counted runs each. The partitions lose 203 to 232 writes, at each hold, and the freeze loses 1 write in each run. [The roadmap](ROADMAP.md#sys-58--valkey-the-accepted-writes-that-a-failover-loses) has the table and the open term of the low edge.

## The second cell: SYS-59

The question: which writes does MongoDB roll back at each write concern?

T11 records the lost writes, but no band judges them. `T11.border-off.66` lost a median of 195 writes with `w: 1`, and `T11.defaults.10` lost none with `w: "majority"`. Both faults are one cut of the primary from the other members, for 40 s. The client stays connected to the old primary. Three new cells make the count a law, with the concern as the knob.

### The terms

Each term comes from the source of MongoDB r9.0.4, in `src/mongo/db`:

- `w: 1` waits for no other member (`write_concern.cpp:428-431`, `write_concern_options.cpp:200-203`).
- `w: "majority"` waits in `awaitReplication` (`write_concern.cpp:434`). That wait fails at the step-down (`repl/replication_coordinator_impl.cpp:2449-2468`).
- Each position from a secondary is an update of that member (`repl/member_data.cpp:159-173`).
- The primary sets a check at the stalest update plus `electionTimeoutMillis` (`repl/replication_coordinator_impl_heartbeat.cpp:1158-1188`).
- A member with no update for that time is stale, and a primary without a majority steps down (`repl/topology_coordinator.cpp:1440-1457`).
- The primary accepts writes until `_stepDownFinish` holds the lock and stops them (`repl/replication_coordinator_impl_heartbeat.cpp:547-641`). It stops the writes in flight (`repl/replication_coordinator_impl.cpp:3901-3917`).
- A voter refuses a candidate with less data (`repl/topology_coordinator.cpp:3755`).
- A heartbeat response from the primary moves the election timer of a secondary (`repl/replication_coordinator_impl_heartbeat.cpp:346-351`).
- After the lift, the fetcher of the old primary finds a diverged oplog (`repl/oplog_fetcher.cpp:1063-1070`). That starts the rollback (`repl/bgsync.cpp:551-554`).
- The rollback finds the common point (`repl/rollback_impl.cpp:1208`), and it recovers to the stable timestamp (`repl/rollback_impl.cpp:699`). Each write after the common point leaves the collection.
- The rollback writes those documents to a file on the disk of that member (`repl/rollback_impl.cpp:684`). The final read does not see that file.

### The law of `w: 1`

The set keeps the history of the new primary. Thus the set loses each write that the old primary acknowledges after the last replicated one.

- The last update of a secondary reached the primary 160 ms to 164 ms before the fault at most, and 2 ms after it at most. That gap is the pause of 50 ms, the round trips, and the two named assumptions below.
- Thus the step-down comes 9.84 s to 10.012 s after the fault. The high edge has a late check of up to 10 ms.
- A cycle is 50 ms at least. It is 72 ms at most: the pause, a round trip of 2 ms, and 10 ms for each of mongod and the client.
- The low edge is the short window at the slow cycle, less one for the phase.
- The high edge is the long window at the fast cycle. That is 201 acknowledgments, and one more that missed its replication at the fault.

This gives **135 to 202 lost writes**, for each hold past the election. These writes are one run of sequence numbers at the end of the history. The band is the one that `lostOf` gave before this item.

### The law of `w: "majority"`

A write that the old primary acknowledges is on one secondary at least. The voters refuse a candidate without it, thus the new primary has it. After the fault, no write gets its majority, and each wait fails at the step-down. The driver retries that write on the new primary. This gives **0 lost writes**.

### The law of a short cut

A cut of 3 s ends before each timer. Each try of TCP doubles its gap, thus the next try after a lift comes by 2 × hold + RTO. That is approximately 6.2 s, and the first election or step-down cannot come before 9.84 s. The members hear each other again, nothing diverges, and no rollback starts. This gives **0 lost writes at `w: 1`**, and no election.

The run must outlast the timers, or the final read comes before the rollback that the law denies. A new optional field, `Cell.watch`, holds the run for 10 s more. The final read comes after 15 s.

Each cell has 4 counted runs in 2 environments. The first write on a new primary has two modes, and 4 runs do not place a median in one band. Thus the band of lost writes judges each cell, as in the partitions of SYS-58.

These cells are not in the set:

- A hold of 80 s. The law predicts the same count, so the cell gives no difference.
- A hold of approximately 5 s to 12 s. Contact can come back on either side of the election, so the law has no band.
- A freeze with `w: 1`. A frozen primary acknowledges nothing, so its count has no term of the concern.

### The assumptions

The band reads two values that no source gives. `executor_slack` is 10 ms and `election_work` is 100 ms, both named in the machine document. The client has its own named slack of 10 ms. The round trip of 2 ms is an assumption of T11.

## The law of SYS-62: the resend

The question: does the resend of ioredis at a reconnect duplicate a write?

The plan said that duplicates are at most the commands in flight at each close. The source keeps that upper bound. It also shows that the bound is loose, and that no fault of T12 reaches it at the defaults.

### The terms of the resend

Each term comes from the source of ioredis at the tag [v6.0.0](https://github.com/redis/ioredis/tree/v6.0.0), in `lib/`:

- `sendCommand` writes a command to the socket, then pushes it on `commandQueue` (`Redis.ts:630-637`).
- A command that `sendCommand` cannot write goes on `offlineQueue` (`Redis.ts:596`). That command was not sent, thus it cannot have a second copy.
- A close from the status `ready` keeps `commandQueue` as `prevCommandQueue` (`redis/event_handler.ts:375-382`).
- At the next `ready`, each command of `prevCommandQueue` goes again, before the offline queue (`redis/event_handler.ts:501-513`, `:519-533`).
- `autoResendUnfulfilledCommands` is true at the default (`redis/RedisOptions.ts:329`). With it off, `prevCommandQueue` becomes null and no promise settles (`redis/event_handler.ts:515`).
- `commandTimeout` rejects a command that did not settle, and the command stays in its queue (`Command.ts:418-426`).
- The 21st close flushes the queues (`redis/event_handler.ts:411-424`, `redis/RedisOptions.ts:337`).
- The socket timer starts at the first command with no timer (`Redis.ts:647-652`), and each reply moves it (`Redis.ts:736-741`). At its end, it destroys the socket (`Redis.ts:725-732`).

The same `Command` object goes again, thus the client sees one `sent` and one `ack` for two copies.

### The law of the copies

A resend adds a copy only if the first copy was applied and it survives. Thus the extra copies are W × c, with W commands in flight at each of c closes from `ready`, if the first copies survive.

That rule removes each fault of T12 at the defaults:

- A `fin` or an `rst` ends the socket with the first copy unread. The data goes with it.
- A partition or a black hole gives no close until the conversion. The first copy then goes with the old primary.
- A freeze gives no close at the defaults, as the frozen kernel keeps the socket. The write gets its reply at the thaw, and `T12.border-off.1` records that path.

The close must come from a timer of the client, while the server holds the first copy unread. `socketTimeout` is that timer.

The doc comment of `socketTimeout` says that the client rejects the running commands (`redis/RedisOptions.ts:47`). The close path has no such step: `closeHandler` keeps them for the resend. The cells test the source against that comment.

A frozen server leaves its kernel running. Each write and each later connect waits in the kernel. Each later connect closes from the status `connect`, thus it does not replace `prevCommandQueue`. So a freeze has one close from `ready`.

At the thaw, a read gives the queued bytes before it gives the end of the stream. Valkey 9.1.2 runs those bytes before a read of 0 bytes frees the client (`src/networking.c:3171-3186`, `:4362`, `:4396`). Thus the first copies are applied, and the resend adds W copies.

If the freeze outlasts the failover, the resend lands on the new primary. The first copies wait in the frozen kernel of the old primary, and its conversion drops them. Thus no copy stays.

A `SET` has no cell. The harness reads the keys as a set, thus each count of copies is 0 by construction, and that cell tests nothing.

### The cells of the resend

Each cell has a freeze of the primary, and 4 counted runs in 2 environments. W is the count of writers, each with one write in flight. The first `Error` is the socket timer, 0.938 s to 1.072 s from the fault.

| Cell | Hold | Client | Extra copies | What the cell shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `T12.resend.1` | 29 s | `socketTimeout` 1 s, W 1 | 1 | The law at W 1 |
| `T12.resend.2` | 29 s | `socketTimeout` 1 s, W 4 | 4 | The law at W 4 |
| `T12.resend.3` | 40 s | `socketTimeout` 1 s, W 4 | 0 | The first copies die with the old primary |
| `T12.resend.4` | 29 s | No timer, W 4 | 0 | The control with no close, with the band of `T12.border-off.1` |
| `T12.resend.5` | 29 s | `socketTimeout` 1 s, resend off, `commandTimeout` 40 s, W 4 | 0 | The control with the resend off |

The 29 s hold stays inside `down-after-milliseconds`, thus no failover comes. With 1 s of `socketTimeout`, a hold of 29 s has at most 10 closes and a hold of 40 s has at most 12, below the 21st.

`T12.resend.3` has one exact band, at 0. A write that reached a replica and got no reply at the freeze gives one copy. That window is approximately 20 µs in each 50 ms. Its reply and its replication are in one pass of `handleClientsWithPendingWrites`. The band stays at 0, because a band of 1 cannot tell the law from the plan's sentence. A copy there is a finding.

`T12.resend.5` needs `commandTimeout`, because a dropped queue settles no promise and each writer waits. Without it, the stream stops. That hang is a fact of the source, and no cell measures it. The timeout must come after the last `ready`, or the resend still finds the commands. `resendCell` throws if it does not.

The harness gets two optional fields: `op` and `window` of the ioredis driver, and a band of extra copies in the prediction of a group. The list `w` keeps each copy, and `writesOf` counts them. A count from a read that found no write is a finding, so a control cannot pass with an empty read.

## The law of SYS-60: the confirms

The question: does a confirmed publish survive a failure of a quorum queue, and does a republish duplicate it?

The plan said that a quorum queue loses no confirmed message, and that duplicates are at most the unconfirmed window. The source keeps both sentences. It also shows two layers of resend, and only one of them adds a copy. The plan's catalog is correct: the cluster of RabbitMQ is in T11.

### The terms of the confirms

Each term comes from the source at the tags [v4.3.6 of rabbitmq-server](https://github.com/rabbitmq/rabbitmq-server/tree/v4.3.6) in `deps/rabbit/src/`, [v3.1.10 of ra](https://github.com/rabbitmq/ra/tree/v3.1.10) in `src/`, and [v2.2.0 of amqplib](https://github.com/amqp-node/amqplib/tree/v2.2.0) in `lib/`.

A confirm comes after a commit on a majority:

- The write-ahead log syncs a batch, then it tells the member that the entries are written (`ra_log_wal.erl:772-783`, `:807`).
- A leader counts only its last written index in the match indexes (`ra_server.erl:3671-3682`).
- The commit index is the index on a majority, and only an entry of the current term can raise it (`ra_server.erl:3648-3657`, `:3684-3688`).
- A member applies each committed command, and the reply goes out as a notification (`ra_server.erl:3250-3282`, `:3469-3479`, `ra_server_proc.erl:2497-2520`).
- A vote goes only to a candidate whose log is up to date (`ra_server.erl:1506`, `:3157-3165`).
- `rabbit_fifo_client` settles each applied command (`rabbit_fifo_client.erl:660-730`). The channel then sends `basic.ack` (`rabbit_channel.erl:2808-2811`, `:2216-2222`, `:2260-2272`).

A leader change adds no copy:

- `rabbit_fifo_client` gives each message an enqueue number, and it keeps the command pending until the notification (`rabbit_fifo_client.erl:170-173`).
- At a `leader_change`, or at a refusal from a follower, it sends each pending command again with the same number (`rabbit_fifo_client.erl:738-760`, `:871-883`, `:1057-1062`).
- `rabbit_fifo` keeps the next number of each enqueuer. It drops a number that it applied, and its reply is `ok` (`rabbit_fifo.erl:1973-1983`, `:2128-2132`).
- A number above the next number gets `not_enqueued`, and that command stays pending (`rabbit_fifo.erl:2122-2127`, `rabbit_fifo_client.erl:838-857`).

A republish on a new connection can add a copy:

- amqplib does not connect again. Its heartbeat ends the connection, and each unconfirmed callback gets the error `channel closed` (`connection.js:332-356`, `:367-376`, `channel.js:36-43`).
- The client of the harness connects to the next node, and it publishes each unconfirmed number again. That is a new channel, thus a new process.
- A new process registers as a new enqueuer, with its own numbers (`rabbit_fifo_client.erl:129-156`). Nothing compares its copy with the first copy.
- A nack adds no copy. A registration that fails rejects the message, and the source says that it was not sent (`rabbit_fifo_client.erl:142-145`, `rabbit_quorum_queue.erl:1350-1358`).

### The law of the copies of a publish

The law of the lost messages is 0. Each confirm comes after a commit on a majority, and each new leader holds each committed entry.

The law of the copies has three parts:

- With no close, the channel lives, and a repeat has its first number. Thus the extra copies are 0 at each leader change.
- With W publishes unconfirmed at one close, the extra copies are W if each first copy was applied. Thus the plan's bound is exact for one close.
- A copy needs its own process, thus c closes give at most W × c copies. The cells have one close.

A freeze of the node of the client gives only the bounds. The frozen node holds each first copy that it did not read. At the thaw, the reader reads the bytes first (`rabbit_reader.erl:484-498`, `:533-542`). The channel then ends, in a race with those commands (`rabbit_channel.erl:276-279`, `:721-751`). Thus a thawed first copy has no exact count, and the band is [0, W].

The exact count needs a fault that keeps each first copy and gives no leader change. The cut `to-clients` does that. The client connects through the node of the leader, and the cut drops only the segments from that node to the client:

- Each publish reaches the leader and gets its commit, and each confirm drops. No election comes.
- amqplib leaves Nagle on at its default (`connect.js:90`, `:152`). With a segment in flight, Nagle holds each later publish until the socket ends. Thus the cells set `noDelay`.
- After a retransmission timeout, the kernel of the client holds each new segment behind the first one. Thus each writer must send first: a wait and a commit stay below `tcp_rto_min`, 200 ms. `confirmCell` throws if they do not.
- The heartbeat must end the connection before the lift. `confirmCell` throws if it can come later.
- The node also ends its side at its own heartbeat (`rabbit_heartbeat.erl:80-85`). That end comes after each commit, thus it changes no count.

## The first count: SYS-67

The question: how many tries does one request cost across hops? Law O08 says that the tries of each hop multiply. The count needs no load generator.

The deepest listener sees `A = t0 × t1 × … × tn` tries for one logical request. Here `ti` is the tries of caller i against the answer of the listener below it. An answer that a caller does not retry gives `ti = 1`, thus it stops the product above it.

Each term comes from a pinned source:

- The `RetryAgent` of undici 8.12.2 has `maxRetries` 5, seven methods and five statuses at its defaults (`lib/handler/retry-handler.js:125-133`).
- Each reply adds one to its counter (`:380`), and each socket error adds one (`:610`). It stops when the counter passes `maxRetries` (`:348`). Thus it sends 6 times.
- It does not retry a method or a status out of its lists (`:332`, `:338`). A close with no reply is in its list (`:144`).
- The `Transport` of Go 1.27.2 ends the round trip at each reply (`src/net/http/transport.go:727-737`). Thus a status costs 1 try.
- On a reused connection, a read failure sends a replayable request again (`:877-881`, `:2510`). A fresh connection does not (`:858`). Thus a GET that meets a close costs 2 tries.
- `retry` of `@mark1russell7/service` 0.6.5 counts the first try in `tries` (`packages/service/src/retrier.ts:121-125`).

The target is one process with a chain of listeners on loopback, each with a real retry client. Each listener writes a line for each arrival, thus the record shows each term. No hop needs a container, and the cell uses no fault. The count reads no network term and no timer, thus a loaded runner cannot move it.
