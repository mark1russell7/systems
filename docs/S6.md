# S6 · The requirements of the service abstraction

This file holds the requirements that `@mark1russell7/service` follows. They come from the first draft of S6, which jarvis keeps. That draft is of 2026-10-05, and it has 94 requirements. They come from the research rounds R1 to R5, and from two digests of an earlier health design by Mark.

This file moves the three groups that the runner follows: the lifecycle (A1 to A14), the health model (B1 to B19) and the configuration as data (E1 to E10). The other groups stay in the draft. Each group moves here with the part of the service that follows it. The status of S6 and its items are in [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md#s6--the-service-abstraction).

## The shape that the service follows

The draft proposes a shape and asks ten questions. Mark answered the first three on 2026-10-05, and Mark gave the other seven to Claude, decided as the draft recommends. The service follows these answers:

- **The declaration is the service.** A builder composes the service before it starts, with one method for each part. `describe()` gives the whole graph as data, and it starts nothing. Thus the declaration and the runtime cannot disagree.
- **A thin runtime.** The runtime takes each knob from the declaration, and it has no defaults of its own. A missing bound is infinite, not 0.
- **Readiness for each controller.** A controller declares its own dependencies. Thus a failed dependency fails only the routes that need it.
- **Two kinds of signal.** Active checks and the outcomes of live traffic are both evidence. A policy reads them and decides which one counts.
- **One timing shape.** Each policy is a debounce of polls or a window of time, and each one converts to the other. The algebra gets the detection bounds from either form.
- **A shutdown in declared phases.** Each owner adds its work to a phase with a cap, and the algebra checks the caps.
- **Machines as documents.** The machines of the runtime are `mark1russell7.machine/1` documents. The tests replay the transitions of the runtime against them.
- **Inequalities, not values.** The service states the inequalities that it needs from the platform. The scenario binds the values: the probes, the preStop delay and the grace period.
- **Wrappers, not patches.** A client is a wrapper of its library. A patch is only for a bug of the library, with a test that removes the patch after the fix.

## Unified or separate: the parts of a client wrapper

Mark chose option B on 2026-10-25. B is a shared core and a thin entry for each driver, with a pressure to unify. Thus each part of a client is in the core of the clients (`packages/service/src/client/`). A part goes into the entry of a driver only when a fact of that driver forces it. This table gives each part, where it is, and the reason for each part that stays separate.

| Part | Where | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The outcome of a call in three values (`Sent`, `Attempt`) and the send options | Core | The outcome is the law (C8). It does not depend on a driver. The gRPC client gives each status code one of the three values. |
| The second try (`sendWithReissue`): only an idempotent operation, one time, in the bound of the request | Core | The rule is the law. The driver gives only a hook (`beforeAgain`) before the second try. The gRPC client gives `beforeAgain` through `readyWait`. The deadline of its second try is the time that is left of the bound. |
| The calls in flight (`inFlight`): each call settles one time, an abort after the send is indeterminate, and the close settles each call | Core | Principle 9 and C10 do not depend on a driver. `idle` gives the wait of a close for its calls. That part is new for the HTTP client, whose agent has no close that waits. The gRPC client tracks each unary call. Its hook `after` cancels a call that the caller abandons or that the close ends, and grpc-js then sends RST_STREAM CANCEL. |
| A call that did not leave the client (`notSent`) | Core | It is a certain error for each driver. The gRPC client also gives it for the five statuses of grpc-js that come before a stream starts. |
| The wait of the bounded close (`withinBound`) | Core | Each close races its work against `close_bound` in the same way. The gRPC client also bounds the wait of its startup for a ready channel with it. |

The gRPC client uses them with no change. It gives `socketEnds` no socket, thus each end of its join is null. |
| The shapes and queries of the meets, the six manifestations, their words, and the detectors of the kernel and of TCP keepalive | Core | The kernel and Node behave the same under each driver. The gRPC client uses them with no change. |

| The problems of a zero, of a whole unit and of a pool (`zeroProblems`, `wholeProblems`, `poolProblems`), and the form of no timer (`orZero`, `wholeMs`, `wholeSeconds`) | Core | pg, MongoDB and amqplib all read 0 as no timer, and each takes some knobs in whole units. These parts moved from pg into the core for MongoDB. RabbitMQ uses them with no change. The HTTP client uses them with no change too. The WebSocket client uses them with no change too. It refuses 0 for `heartbeat_interval` and `keepalive_idle`, and it takes `keepalive_idle` in whole seconds. The gRPC client uses `zeroProblems` and `wholeProblems` with no change. It refuses 0 for `keepalive_time`, and it takes each timer of grpc-js in whole milliseconds. |
| The wait for a ready client before the second try (`readyWait`) | Core | ioredis and the RabbitMQ client both connect again by themselves. Thus a second try waits for `ready`, in the time that is left of the bound. This part moved from Redis into the core for RabbitMQ. The WebSocket client connects again by itself too, and it uses this part with no change. The grpc-js driver connects again by itself too, thus the gRPC client uses this part with no change. |
| The backoff of the tries to connect (`retryBackoff`, `RetryKnobs`, `retryProblems`) | Core | Redis, RabbitMQ and the WebSocket client all try again after a delay that doubles from `retry_base` to `retry_cap`, with a jitter up to the base. These parts moved from Redis into the core. The Redis entry keeps its export of `retryBackoff`. The WebSocket client uses the same backoff. The grpc-js driver has a backoff of its own: 1.6 times at each step, with a jitter of 20 %. The gRPC client gives it `retry_base` and `retry_cap`, and it uses only `RetryKnobs` and `retryProblems`. |
| The problem O07 (`deadlineProblem`): the deadline on the server is shorter than the request timeout | Core | pg and MongoDB both have a deadline on the server. Each driver gives the noun of its call: a statement or a command. The gRPC client does not use this part. The grpc-js driver sends its deadline to the server as `grpc-timeout`, thus the server has the same deadline. |
| The timer of each call of the wrapper (`trackWithTimer`): at its bound, the call is indeterminate | Core | amqplib has no timer for a confirm or for an RPC. The agent of Node has no timer for a request. Thus both wrappers end a call at `request_timeout`. This part moved from RabbitMQ into the core for the HTTP client. The other drivers have a timer of their own. ws has no timer for a reply or for a pong, thus the WebSocket client uses this part too. The grpc-js driver has a deadline of its own for each call. Thus the gRPC client gives it the time that is left, and it does not use this part. |
| The problems of a timer of the wrapper, of a connection age and of a keepalive that is always on (`wrapperTimerProblems`, `ageProblems`, `keepaliveOnProblems`) | Core | A timer of the wrapper must be a finite time above zero, because it is the only bound. RabbitMQ, MongoDB and the HTTP client all refuse a finite connection age. MongoDB and the agent of Node always turn keepalive on. These parts moved from RabbitMQ and MongoDB into the core, with the same texts. The WebSocket client uses `wrapperTimerProblems` and `ageProblems` with no change. ws cannot replace a connection at an age. The gRPC client uses `wrapperTimerProblems` for `connect_bound` and `request_timeout`, and `ageProblems`, with no change. The grpc-js driver cannot replace a connection at an age. |
| The problem O01 (`idleCloseProblem`): the pool closes an idle connection before its server does | Core | The rule is the law, and it does not depend on a driver. The HTTP client is the first client that knows the idle close of its server. The gRPC client uses it for `client_idle_timeout`. For the gRPC client, this part moved to a general form: it takes the name of the idle knob. Its default keeps the text of the HTTP client. The tests of the HTTP client pass with no change. |
| The schedule of the tries to connect (`retrySchedule`): the count of the tries, one timer, and a reset at the join | Core | amqplib and ws do not connect again by themselves. Thus the RabbitMQ and WebSocket clients both schedule the next try on the clock. This part moved from RabbitMQ into the core for the WebSocket client. The grpc-js driver connects again by itself, thus the gRPC client does not use this part. |
| The problem of a count of misses (`missesProblems`) | Core | The watchdog of Redis and the heartbeat of the WebSocket client both end a connection after a count of misses. This part moved from Redis into the core, with the same text. The gRPC client does not use this part. The grpc-js driver ends the transport at the first PING with no ACK. |
| The judge of an HTTP/1.1 status line (`statusLineJudge`) and the authority of a host and a port (`authority`) | Core | The dial of the HTTP client and the upgrade of the WebSocket client both read a status line, and both write a Host header. These parts moved from the HTTP client into the core, with the same texts. The gRPC client uses `authority` for its target, with no change. It reads no status line. |
| The bytes of the dial | Driver | Redis answers an inline PING with `+PONG`. PostgreSQL answers an SSLRequest with one byte, before the authentication. MongoDB answers an OP_MSG hello to `admin` with an OP_MSG reply. RabbitMQ answers the protocol header of AMQP 0-9-1 with `connection.start`, before the authentication. An HTTP server answers `OPTIONS *` with a status line. A server that does not route `*` still answers with a status. A WebSocket server answers the upgrade GET with a status line. The dial of the WebSocket client accepts only 101. A gRPC server answers the HTTP/2 preface with a SETTINGS frame. The dial of the gRPC client accepts only a SETTINGS frame with no ACK, on stream 0. |
| How a failure becomes an outcome | Driver | ioredis gives a `ReplyError`, and a text for a command that it did not write. pg gives a severity and an SQLSTATE. A FATAL ends the session, thus its outcome is not certain. The MongoDB driver gives a class of error by name. An interrupted command (codes 91, 189, 11600 and 11602) and a write concern error are not certain. amqplib names few classes of error, thus the wrapper reads the fields. A reply code of the broker is certain. A nack, and the end of the channel before the confirm or the reply, are not certain. `IllegalOperationError` (a closed channel) did not send the call. The agent of Node gives a whole response, or an error with no class. Each whole response is `ok`, with its status. A failure before the request has a connected socket did not send it. A failure after that, and a body that ends early, are not certain. ws gives an error with no class, and a close code. A reply with an error is certain. A failed write, a timeout and the end of the generation are not certain. The grpc-js driver gives a status code and its details. A code that tells a refusal is certain. UNAVAILABLE, INTERNAL and DEADLINE_EXCEEDED come both before and after the send. Thus the gRPC client reads the details of five statuses of grpc-js that come before a stream starts. |
| The method that sends, and the default table of operations | Driver | A Redis command names itself, thus `REDIS_OPERATIONS` gives its idempotency. SQL text does not tell if a statement changes a state, thus a pg query names its operation, and pg has no default table. A MongoDB command document does not tell it either, thus `command` names its operation too. A message does not tell if its consumers remove duplicates. Thus `publish` and `get` of RabbitMQ name their operation, and RabbitMQ has no default table. An HTTP method tells its idempotency by RFC 9110. Thus `HTTP_OPERATIONS` gives a default for each method, and a spec can add a keyed operation. A WebSocket message does not tell its idempotency either. Thus `request` names its operation. `WS_OPERATIONS` gives only `ping`, and a ping is idempotent. A gRPC method tells its idempotency in its proto (`idempotency_level`). Thus `grpcOperations` makes a table from the levels. `GRPC_OPERATIONS` gives only the Check of the health service. |
| The wait before the second try | Driver | ioredis connects again by itself, thus the second try waits for `ready` through `readyWait` of the core. The RabbitMQ client connects again by itself too, and it waits in the same way. The pg pool opens a new connection at the checkout, thus it does not wait. The MongoDB driver waits for a writable server in its selection, thus its call bound is `client_retry_window` plus `request_timeout`. The agent opens a new socket for the second try of the HTTP client, thus that client does not wait. The WebSocket client connects again by itself too, and it waits through `readyWait` in the same way. The grpc-js driver connects again by itself too, thus the gRPC client waits through `readyWait` in the same way. |
| What settles the calls in flight before the close | Driver | ioredis has one connection, and its close settles each command on it. Each pg query has its own client of the pool, and that client fails its own query. The close of the MongoDB driver fails each command on a connection that it checked out. The end of a RabbitMQ generation fails each confirm and each RPC on its channel. The client settles what stays one turn later, thus a reply code of amqplib arrives first. The agent has no close that waits. The HTTP client waits for its calls through `idle` of the core, in `close_bound`. At the bound, `settleAll` of the core settles each call. The end of a WebSocket generation fails each request and each ping on it. The close of the WebSocket client waits for its calls through `idle`, in `close_bound`. The grpc-js driver ends each call in flight with UNAVAILABLE when its transport ends. A GOAWAY lets each call end on the old transport. The close of the gRPC client waits for its calls through `idle`, in `close_bound`. |
| The release of a client after a query that is not certain | Driver (pg) | pg keeps a timed-out query active on the server. The client goes back with an error, thus the pool ends it. ioredis has no checkout. The MongoDB driver closes the connection itself after a network error or an abort. RabbitMQ has no pool. A late reply to `basic.get` has its own row. |
| The offline queue | Driver (Redis) | ioredis has an offline queue, and the wrapper turns it off. A command while the client is not ready then fails at once, as not sent. pg has no offline queue. A MongoDB command waits in the selection, in a bound. amqplib has no offline queue. A RabbitMQ call while the client is not ready fails at once, as not sent. The agent has a queue for a full pool. A request in that queue did not leave the client, thus a close, an abort or `request_timeout` there gives not sent. ws throws a send while it connects, and it has no queue. A WebSocket call while the client is not ready fails at once, as not sent. The grpc-js driver holds a call while its channel connects. The gRPC client refuses a call while it is not ready, as not sent. In TRANSIENT_FAILURE, grpc-js fails a call at once, as not sent. |
| The watchdog, its knobs and `heartbeatMisses` | Driver (Redis, WebSocket) | A PING tests one connection. A pool does not tell which idle client a PING gets, thus pg ends idle clients at `pool_idle_expiry` in its place. The MongoDB driver has its own monitor, with a hello at each `heartbeat_interval`. The heartbeat of amqplib ends a silent connection 2 to 3 heartbeats after its last data. Thus the RabbitMQ client has no watchdog. The free socket timer of the agent closes an idle socket at `pool_idle_expiry`, thus the HTTP client has no watchdog. The WebSocket client has a heartbeat with the same `heartbeat()`: a ping frame at each `heartbeat_interval`, one at a time. ws has no heartbeat of its own. The grpc-js driver has a keepalive of its own: an HTTP/2 PING at each `keepalive_time`, and the end of the transport at `keepalive_timeout`. Thus the gRPC client has no watchdog. |
| The schedule of the tries to connect again (`retryStrategy`, the retry window, the revive, and the open of a generation) | Driver (Redis, RabbitMQ, WebSocket, gRPC) | ioredis connects again by itself, and the wrapper gives it a `retryStrategy`. The recovery of amqplib stays off, thus the RabbitMQ client opens each generation itself, with a timer of its own. pg does not connect again, and the next checkout opens a connection. The MongoDB monitor connects again by itself. The backoff of Redis and RabbitMQ is in the core. The agent does not connect again by itself, and the next request of the HTTP client opens a new socket. ws does not connect again by itself. Thus the WebSocket client opens each generation itself, through `retrySchedule` of the core. The grpc-js driver connects again by itself, after its own backoff. An IDLE channel connects only for a call, thus the gRPC client asks it to connect at once. |
| The states and the transitions of the machine document | Driver | The states of ioredis are the states of one connection. The state of the pg client is the outcome of the last connect of its pool. The state of the MongoDB client is the view of its topology: a writable server gives `ready`. The state of the RabbitMQ client is its generation: one connection and one confirm channel. A resource alarm gives `blocked`. The state of the HTTP client is the outcome of the last connect of a socket, as for pg. A failed request on a reused socket records `reuse.failed`, and the state stays. The state of the WebSocket client is its generation: one connection after its upgrade. The last miss of the heartbeat gives `failing`. The state of the gRPC client is the connectivity state of its channel. Each READY is one generation, and TRANSIENT_FAILURE gives `failing`. |
| The steps of the close | Driver | ioredis closes with a QUIT, then a disconnect, then a destroy of its socket. pg ends its pool, then destroys the socket of each client that stays. MongoDB closes its client and records `close.bound` at the bound, because the driver gives no socket to destroy. RabbitMQ sends `connection.close`, then destroys the socket at the bound. Behind a black hole, close-ok does not come. The HTTP client waits for its calls in flight, then destroys each socket with `agent.destroy()`. The WebSocket client waits for its calls in flight, then it sends a close frame and waits for the close. At the bound, `terminate()` destroys the socket. The gRPC client waits for its calls in flight. At the bound, it cancels each call that stays, and then it closes the channel. |
| The form of "no timer" in the options | Driver (Redis, RabbitMQ, gRPC) | ioredis reads undefined or false. pg and MongoDB read 0, and the core gives `orZero` for them. amqplib reads 0 too. The RabbitMQ client writes its heartbeat in the URL in whole seconds, with 0 for "infinite". The agent reads a `timeout` of 0 as no free socket timer, and then the hint of the server does not apply. Thus the HTTP client refuses 0 and "infinite" for `pool_idle_expiry`. ws reads a `handshakeTimeout` of 0 as no timer. The WebSocket client gives it `connect_bound`, and gives keepalive only when `keepalive_idle` is finite. The grpc-js driver reads no `grpc.keepalive_time_ms` as no PING. Thus the gRPC client leaves that option out for "infinite". Each other timer of grpc-js must be finite. |
| The problems of a spec, other than the time, the zero and the whole unit of each knob | Driver | ioredis cannot replace a connection at an age. A stricter Redis check refuses a spec that 0.5 accepts, and that is a breaking change. MongoDB refuses a heartbeat below 500 ms, a keepalive of zero and a finite connection age. RabbitMQ refuses a finite connection age. It also refuses a `request_timeout` that is not finite, because the timer of the wrapper is the only bound of a call. The HTTP client refuses a `hint_buffer` that is not finite, because the agent subtracts it from the hint of the server. The WebSocket client refuses a password and a path with no leading `/`. With more than one miss, it refuses a `heartbeat_timeout` at or above its `heartbeat_interval`. The gRPC client refuses an idle timeout below 1 s, a TCP keepalive and a password. |
| The fields of a spec that name the options of the driver (`db` and `username`, against `database`, `user` and `pool`) | Driver | They are the names of each driver. A rename of the Redis fields breaks the users of 0.5. MongoDB adds `authSource`, `replicaSet` and `seeds`. RabbitMQ adds `vhost` and `seeds`. The HTTP client adds `pool.max` and the `check` request. The WebSocket client adds `path`, `protocols`, `headers` and `replies`. The gRPC client adds `keepaliveWithoutCalls`, `metadata` and `healthService`. |
| The knobs that only one driver has | Driver | Only ioredis has `socketTimeout`. Only MongoDB has a wait queue for a checkout (`checkout_timeout`). The RabbitMQ client has no knob that another client does not have. Only the HTTP client has `hint_buffer` and `server_idle_close`. Their rows follow. Only the WebSocket client has `heartbeat_timeout`, the bound of one ping of its heartbeat. Only the gRPC client has `keepalive_time`, `keepalive_timeout`, `client_idle_timeout` and `server_keepalive_min_time`. It has `server_idle_close` too, as the HTTP client does. |
| The server's id in a join, and when the join occurs | Driver | Redis gives `CLIENT ID` at `ready`. PostgreSQL gives the process id of the backend at the startup. MongoDB gives the connection id of the server (`serverConnectionId`) with the first command of a connection, and no local socket. RabbitMQ gives no id in a frame. When the channel opens, the client records the name of the connection, `local:port -> remote:port`, as the broker names it. HTTP gives no id of a connection. The HTTP client records the ends of each new socket of its pool when that socket connects. WebSocket gives no id of a connection either. The WebSocket client records the ends of its socket and the subprotocol at `open`. gRPC gives no id of a connection, and grpc-js gives no socket. The gRPC client records the target at each READY, with null ends. |
| The list of meetings and the detectors from the timers of the driver | Driver | The timers of each driver differ. The words of `app-stall` name an example of each server. MongoDB finds a silent server with the write-off of its monitor. RabbitMQ finds a silent broker with its heartbeat, and a resource alarm with `connection.blocked`. The HTTP client finds a silent idle socket with the free socket timer of its agent. The WebSocket client finds a silent server with its heartbeat. The library of a stalled server still answers each ping, thus `request_timeout` finds an app stall. The gRPC client finds a silent server with the PING of grpc-js. At the starting knobs, it sends a PING only with a call in flight. With no call, `client_idle_timeout` finds a silent server: the new connect does not become READY. |
| The `checkout` step | Driver (pg) | Only pg gives a checkout call (`pool.connect`). The MongoDB driver checks out a connection only inside a command. |
| The loader of the driver | Driver | ioredis gives its client as the default export. pg gives `Pool` in its default export. MongoDB gives `MongoClient` as a named export. amqplib gives `connect` as a named export of CommonJS. `node:http` is a built-in, thus the HTTP client has no peer dependency. It loads `Agent` and `request` at the connect. ws gives `WebSocket` as its default export. The WebSocket client loads it at the connect. `@grpc/grpc-js` gives `Client` as a named export of CommonJS. The gRPC client loads it at the connect, with identity serializers: the caller encodes each message. |
| The starting knobs (`PG_STARTING_KNOBS`, `MONGO_STARTING_KNOBS`, `RABBIT_STARTING_KNOBS`, `HTTP_CLIENT_STARTING_KNOBS`, `WS_CLIENT_STARTING_KNOBS`, `GRPC_CLIENT_STARTING_KNOBS`) | Driver | Each value is a measurement of the cells on one driver. Redis has no starting knobs yet. The WebSocket values come from the WS cells on ws 8.22.0. The gRPC values come from the gRPC cells on grpc-js 1.14.6. |
| The retries of the driver (`retryReads` and `retryWrites` are false, and `grpc.enable_retries` is 0) | Driver (MongoDB, gRPC) | The MongoDB driver sends a command again by itself. The wrapper turns that off, thus only the core decides a second send. The grpc-js driver has a transparent retry, and a retry policy from the service config of the resolver. The gRPC client turns both off, with `grpc.service_config_disable_resolution` too. |
| `maxTimeMS` in the document of each command | Driver (MongoDB) | `db.command` does not add `maxTimeMS` from its options. Thus the wrapper writes the deadline on the server in the document. |
| The monitor mode `stream` and the time of the write-off | Driver (MongoDB) | The write-off of a silent server comes at `connect_bound` plus `heartbeat_interval` after its last answer. The client derives `driver_writeoff_time` from them. |
| One generation: a connection and its confirm channel, and the end of either one | Driver (RabbitMQ) | In amqplib a channel can end alone. A reply code closes the channel and keeps the connection. Thus the client closes that connection, and opens a new generation. |
| The state `blocked` | Driver (RabbitMQ) | Only RabbitMQ blocks a connection at a resource alarm, and it tells the client with `connection.blocked`. The broker then reads no publish, and it still sends its heartbeats. |
| A message that comes after its `basic.get` ended goes back to its queue (`nack` with requeue) | Driver (RabbitMQ) | Only `basic.get` gives the caller a message that the broker holds for it. Nobody settles a late reply, thus the client gives it back at once. |
| The jobs of a queue drain (`jobs`), and the count of deliveries (`deliveryCount`) | Driver (RabbitMQ) | Only RabbitMQ is a queue here. A quorum queue counts the deliveries in `x-delivery-count`. A settle on an ended generation throws, because the broker put the message back by itself. |
| The connect timer of the wrapper, and the bound of the queue of the agent | Driver (HTTP) | The agent has no connect timer, and a request in its queue has no bound. Thus the client arms `connect_bound` when the agent gives a new socket. It ends a request that waits for a socket at `request_timeout`. |
| The first request at the connect | Driver (HTTP) | The agent opens a socket only for a request. Thus the connect sends the check request, and the first socket opens. pg opens its first connection with a checkout in the same way, but pg has a checkout call. |
| The hint of the server and `hint_buffer` (`agentKeepAliveTimeoutBuffer`) | Driver (HTTP) | The agent closes a free socket at the smaller of `timeout` and the hint of the server (`Keep-Alive: timeout=`) less its buffer. A hint at or below the buffer gives no reuse. No other driver reads a hint from its server. |
| `server_idle_close` is a knob of the spec, and not of the declaration | Driver (HTTP, gRPC) | The knob is the idle close of the server. The scenario binds it as `close@server`. Thus the client uses it only for O01 in `problems`. The gRPC client uses it in the same way, against `client_idle_timeout`. |
| The record `reuse.failed` | Driver (HTTP) | Only the agent gives a free socket that the server can close at the same time: the keep-alive race. The record tells that race from a reset of a busy socket. |
| A status of 500 or more fails the round-trip check, and so does a health reply that is not SERVING | Driver (HTTP, gRPC) | Only an HTTP server tells a failure of its own with a status, and keeps its connection. The call is `ok`, but the check measures the server. A gRPC health service answers NOT_SERVING as a reply, thus the call is `ok` too. The round-trip check of the gRPC client passes only for SERVING. |
| The next node at each try (`seeds`) | Driver (RabbitMQ) | amqplib connects to one URL. Thus the client gives each try the next node. The MongoDB driver takes its seeds in its URL, and the Redis and pg clients have one node. |
| The match of a reply to its request (`replies`) | Driver (WebSocket) | A WebSocket message has no reply of its own. Thus a spec gives `replies`, which reads the id of a request in a message. A message with no match goes to `onMessage`. |
| The match of a pong by a sequence in its payload | Driver (WebSocket) | RFC 6455 §5.5.3 lets a pong answer only the latest ping. Thus each ping frame carries a sequence, and a pong settles each ping up to its sequence. |
| `terminate()` at the end of a generation | Driver (WebSocket) | The close of ws only writes a close frame, and a silent server does not answer it. In the cells, `terminate()` ended the socket in 0.3 to 1 ms. |
| The adapter that holds the events of a connection until the client listens (`adaptWs`) | Driver (WebSocket) | ws can emit `error` and `close` before the client listens. In Node, an `error` event with no listener throws. Thus the adapter listens at once. |
| TCP keepalive through the options of `net.connect` | Driver (WebSocket) | ws sets no TCP keepalive. Thus the client gives `keepAlive` and its delay to ws, and ws gives them to `net.connect`. |
| `connect_bound` bounds only the wait of the startup | Driver (gRPC) | The grpc-js driver has no connect timeout, and the wrapper cannot stop one connect of grpc-js. A connect into a black hole stays CONNECTING until the kernel gives up. |
| One channel for the life of the client, and a connect at once at IDLE | Driver (gRPC) | The grpc-js driver owns its connection: a channel connects, connects again and goes IDLE by itself. An IDLE channel connects only for a call, thus the client asks it at once. |

| The longest timer of Node | Driver (gRPC) | The grpc-js driver gives each of its timers to `setTimeout` with no check. A delay above 2147483647 ms starts after 1 ms. Thus the gRPC client refuses a longer timer. |

### A call of the RabbitMQ client

A call of the RabbitMQ client is one of two things. It is a publish on a confirm channel until its confirm, or an RPC until its reply. The RPCs are `basic.get` and the passive declare of the round-trip check.

- An ack is `ok`. A nack is indeterminate, because a route with more than one queue can put the message on some of them.
- A reply code of the broker is a certain error. A call while the client is not ready, while it is blocked, or on a closed channel did not leave the client.
- A timeout, an abort, and the end of the channel or of the connection before the confirm or the reply are indeterminate.
- `ack` and `nack` of a delivery are frames with no reply, thus they are not calls. The end of their generation puts the message back on its queue: at least once.

The defaults come from the cells of R3, on amqplib 2.2.0 against RabbitMQ 4.3.6:

- `request_timeout` is 9.5 s. After a loss of the leader of a quorum queue, the first confirm came at 6.61 s, 6.68 s and 8.05 s (T11). The timeout also fits in the slowest poll of 10 s (O13).
- `heartbeat_interval` is 15 s. The heartbeat ended a silent connection at 45.009 s, before the kernel gave up at 53.016 s (O17). At the 60 s of the broker, it came at 180.009 s. A frame each 7.5 s keeps a flow through a router that forgets it at 30 s (O03).
- `connect_bound` is 5 s (DP44). `retry_base` is 1 s, because the cells connected to the next node 1 s after the end of a connection.

### A call of the HTTP client

A call of the HTTP client is one request until the end of the body of its response. The client keeps the whole body. The agent gives the socket back to its pool only at the end of the response. A body that ends early is not certain.

- A whole response is `ok`, with its status in the value. RFC 9110 does not say that a 5xx response did not apply, thus the client does not make it an error. The caller decides.
- A request leaves the client when it has a connected socket: a free socket that the agent reuses, or a new socket after its connect.
- Before that, the request did not leave the client. That is so in the queue of the agent, in a connect, at a refusal, at `connect_bound`, and at an abort or a close there.
- After that, a failure, a timeout and an abort are indeterminate. The client then destroys the socket, thus no later request gets a part of that response.

Idempotency comes from the method, by RFC 9110 §9.2.2. GET, HEAD, OPTIONS, TRACE, PUT and DELETE are idempotent, and POST and PATCH are not. The operation of a call is its method in upper case, or the `operation` that the call names. A spec can add a keyed operation, for example `POST /charge` with an `Idempotency-Key` header.

The keep-alive race is the reason for the second try. A server closes an idle connection at S. A request that goes on that socket between S − RTT and S gets a reset. Go sends a GET again on a new connection, and not a POST. The core gives the same rule: the client sends a GET again one time on a new socket, and not a POST.

The defaults come from the HTTP cells, on the agent of Node 24.21.0 against a Node server at its defaults:

- `pool_idle_expiry` is 4 s, and `hint_buffer` is 1 s. The server sends a hint of 5 s, and it closes an idle connection at 6 s (6.0003 s in the cells). The agent then closes a free socket at 4 s, below the close of the server (O01).
- `request_timeout` is 9.5 s, in the slowest poll of 10 s (O13). The agent has no timer of a call.
- `connect_bound` is 5 s (DP44). `keepalive_idle` is 1 s, the default of the agent.

### A call of the WebSocket client

A call of the WebSocket client is one of two things. It is a request message until its reply, or a ping frame until its pong.

- WebSocket has no reply of its own. Thus `request(operation, { id, data })` writes one message, and `replies` of the spec reads the id of a reply in each message.
- A reply is `ok`. A reply that carries an error is a certain error. A message that answers no request goes to `onMessage`. A late reply goes to nobody.
- A call did not leave the client when the client is not ready, when ws throws, or when an id is already in flight. A spec with no `replies` sends no request.
- A failed write, `request_timeout`, an abort after the write, and the end of the generation are indeterminate. The server can have applied the request.
- `ping()` writes a ping frame with a sequence in its payload. A pong settles each ping up to its sequence, because a pong can answer only the latest ping.
- `send()` writes a message with no reply, thus it is not a call. It gives true when ws took the message.

A message does not tell its idempotency. Thus each request names its operation. `WS_OPERATIONS` gives only `ping`, which is idempotent. The second try of an idempotent operation waits for a ready client through `readyWait`.

The heartbeat is the `heartbeat()` of the package, as in Redis. It sends one ping at a time, and `terminate()` destroys the socket at the last miss.

The defaults come from the WS cells, on ws 8.22.0:

- `heartbeat_interval` is 4 s. A heartbeat of 4 s keeps a flow through a router that forgets it at 5 s, and 6 s does not (O03).
- `heartbeat_timeout` is 3 s. A stall of 1 s with a timeout of 3 s ended no connection. The detection is 4 s + 3 s = 7 s.
- `heartbeatMisses` is 1. A heartbeat of 2 s, 1 s and one miss ended a silent connection 0.999 to 1.0035 s after the ping (495 runs).
- The kernel of a frozen server still acknowledges the ping, thus TCP does not find a frozen server. Only the pong does.
- `retry_base` is 500 ms and `retry_cap` is 4 s, as in the cells. `close_bound` is 2 s, because a silent server does not answer a close frame.
- `request_timeout` is 9.5 s, in the slowest poll of 10 s (O13). `connect_bound` is 5 s (DP44). ws sets no keepalive, thus `keepalive_idle` is infinite.

### A call of the gRPC client

A call of the gRPC client is one unary call until its status. `call(method, request)` sends the bytes of one request message. An OK status gives the bytes of the response message, and the caller decodes them.

- A stream is not a call. A stream gives many messages, and its status comes only at its end. The core gives one outcome to one call, thus the client sends no stream.
- The deadline of each try is the time that is left in `request_timeout`. The grpc-js driver sends it to the server as `grpc-timeout`, and it ends the call at the deadline.
- The client refuses a call while it is not ready, as not sent. A call that the caller abandons, or that the close ends, gets RST_STREAM CANCEL.
- The round-trip check is a unary Check of the health service (grpc.health.v1). It passes only for SERVING.

The outcome comes from the status code. For three codes, it also comes from the details text of grpc-js 1.14.6:

| Status | Outcome | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OK (0) | `ok` | The server sent its response. |
| INVALID_ARGUMENT (3), NOT_FOUND (5), ALREADY_EXISTS (6), PERMISSION_DENIED (7), FAILED_PRECONDITION (9), ABORTED (10), OUT_OF_RANGE (11), UNIMPLEMENTED (12), UNAUTHENTICATED (16) | Certain error | The server or a proxy refused the call, and did not apply it. A proxy gives 401, 403 and 404 as UNAUTHENTICATED, PERMISSION_DENIED and UNIMPLEMENTED. |
| UNAVAILABLE (14) "No connection established", "Name resolution failed" or "Stream refused by server" | Not sent | The grpc-js driver gives the first two before a pick of a transport. REFUSED_STREAM tells that the server did not process the stream (RFC 9113 §8.7). |
| INTERNAL (13) "Failed to start HTTP/2 stream" | Not sent | The stream did not start on the transport. |
| DEADLINE_EXCEEDED (4) with "Waiting for LB pick" or "waiting for name resolution" | Not sent | The deadline came before the call had a transport. |
| CANCELLED (1), UNKNOWN (2), DEADLINE_EXCEEDED (4), RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED (8), INTERNAL (13), UNAVAILABLE (14) and DATA_LOSS (15), with other details | Indeterminate | These statuses can come after the server applied the call. Examples are "Connection dropped", a deadline with `remote_addr`, a RST_STREAM CANCEL and ENHANCE_YOUR_CALM. |

Idempotency comes from the method. A proto gives each method an `idempotency_level`. `grpcOperations` makes the table: NO_SIDE_EFFECTS and IDEMPOTENT are idempotent. IDEMPOTENCY_UNKNOWN, the default of a proto, is not. A method that the table does not name is not sent again.

The retries of grpc-js are off. The grpc-js driver has a transparent retry, and a retry policy that the service config of the resolver can turn on. A retry of grpc-js does not know the table of the client, and it is not in the count of tries. Thus `grpc.enable_retries` is 0, and `grpc.service_config_disable_resolution` is 1. Only the core sends a call again: an idempotent method, one time, in the bound.

The client has one channel for its life, and each READY of the channel is one generation. The grpc-js driver connects again by itself after its backoff. A channel goes IDLE after a GOAWAY, a reset, a FIN, the keepalive or `client_idle_timeout`. An IDLE channel connects only for a call, thus the client asks it to connect at once. Readiness is then earned again.

When its transport ends, grpc-js ends each call in flight with UNAVAILABLE. A GOAWAY lets each call in flight end on the old transport.

The defaults come from the gRPC cells, on grpc-js 1.14.6 against grpc-go v1.84.0, etcd and four proxies:

- `request_timeout` is 9.5 s, in the slowest poll of 10 s (O13). In DP44, a deadline of 4 s ended each call at 4.003 s to 4.004 s.
- `keepalive_time` is 300 s and `keepalive_timeout` is 20 s (DP24). The grpc-go server permits one PING each 5 min at its defaults (O26). The transport of a silent server ended at 320.005 s.
- Against etcd, with a `MinTime` of 5 s, a keepalive of 60 s ended a silent transport at 80.012 s. A PING each 4 s got GOAWAY too_many_pings at the 4th PING.
- `keepaliveWithoutCalls` is false. The grpc-go server refuses a PING with no call in flight at its defaults (`PermitWithoutStream` false).
- `client_idle_timeout` is 60 s. The proxies ended an idle connection with GOAWAY at 75 s (nginx), 180 s (Traefik), 300 s (Caddy) and 1 h (Envoy). The client closes first (O01).
- At a GOAWAY, the channel goes IDLE, the calls in flight end on the old transport, and the client connects again at once.
- `retry_base` is 1 s, the first backoff of grpc-js in T07, with no jitter. `retry_cap` is 4 s, thus the most delay is 4.8 s, in the slowest poll.
- `connect_bound` is 5 s (DP44). The grpc-js driver has no connect timeout, thus this knob bounds only the wait of the startup. `close_bound` is 2 s.

A new client of S1 must use the core. It extends the shapes, it sends through `sendWithReissue` and `inFlight`, and it gives its checks through `stepChecks`. For each part that it keeps in its own entry, it adds a row to this table. The row gives the fact of the driver that forces the part. A part without such a fact moves into the core.

A sixth incident has cells: a flapping dependency. The cells apply the black hole of the shared dependency three times in one run, and heal it after each fault. The runner stamps each fault and each heal, and the judge gives each cycle its own window.

## The probe load of the session check

The probe load is the traffic that a candidate adds to find a stuck connection. The readiness probe of the kubelet (2 s, 3 times) and the poll of the service are the same for each candidate. Thus only the PINGs of D differ. A, B and C send no PING by their code. HTTP/1.1 has no PING frame, and `node:http2` sends a PING only when its caller asks for one. Their score is 0, from the code (`derived`), with no run.

The bound of D comes from its code. The check sends a PING only at a tick of `keepalive_time` (0.5 s). The session must have a stream in flight and no frame from the peer since the tick before. The ACK of a PING does not count as a frame, thus a quiet session with a stream sends a PING at each tick.

| Session | PINGs each second | Bytes on the wire each second |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Busy: a frame from the peer in each tick | 0 | 0 |
| Idle: no stream in flight (`keepaliveWithoutCalls` is false) | 0 | 0 |
| Quiet with a stream in flight, and the peer answers each PING | 2 at the most (1 / `keepalive_time`) | 34 at the most in each direction, 138 with the headers of TCP and IP |
| Quiet with a stream in flight, and the peer does not answer | 1, then the check closes the session | 17 once |

A PING frame and its ACK each have a header of 9 bytes and 8 bytes of data (RFC 9113, sections 4.1 and 6.7). The headers of TCP and IP add 52 bytes to each frame that has its own segment.

The prediction of each cell of D comes before its run, in `pingsOf` of `packages/cells/src/callers.ts`. It gives the PINGs of the 3 clients in each counted run:

| Incident | iptables | IPVS | nftables | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A pod that cannot serve | 0 | 0 | 0 | Each replica answers each stream at once. |
| A failed prober | 0 | 0 | 0 | The target still serves each stream. |
| A partitioned pod | 0 to 3 | 0 to 3 | 0 to 3 | One PING for each session on the target. A next session on the target does not connect, thus it sends no PING. |
| A stalled pod | 0 to 9 | 0 to 3 | 0 to 9 | One PING for each session on the target, and a next session on it also connects. The random picks are negative binomial. |
| A black-holed shared dependency | 3 to 126 | 3 to 126 | 3 to 126 | Each stream waits for Redis until the latch trips (8 s to 20 s), and the server answers each PING. Each client sends a PING at each tick until the trip, 42 at the most. |

The image of the clients counts each PING of a check and each ACK, and it writes one line each second. Commit 47909ad gave each band before the run.

The kind run [40440516571](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/40440516571) gives the measure. Each of the 15 cells of D held in 3 of 3 runs, and each count of PINGs is in its band. No band changed after the run.

The table gives the PINGs of the 3 clients in each counted run. In the partition and the stall, no PING got an ACK, thus each PING closed its session. In the shared dependency, each PING got its ACK, apart from 3 of 382 that a close of the session ended first.

The most PINGs were 51 in a run of 41 s: 17 for each client, or 0.42 for each session each second. The bound is 2 for each session each second, only while the session is quiet with a stream in flight. That is 138 bytes each second in each direction at the most, with the headers of TCP and IP. The probe load is thus not a cost that matters, and it does not block a default.

The check stays an opt-in, and its default does not change. This table gives each reason.

| Reason | The evidence |
| --- | --- |
| The package has no client of HTTP/2 to hold a default | `sessionCheck` is a part that a client of HTTP/2 uses on its own session. The HTTP client of the package (`./http-client`) is the agent of Node, on HTTP/1.1. |
| No caller of the fleet uses a client of HTTP/2 | A default that no caller reads changes no behaviour, and it adds a part that no cell of a caller tests. |
| The service has no defaults: each knob is explicit (E2) | The gRPC client gets its keepalive PING through its knobs. A client of HTTP/2 gets the check the same way, with `keepalive_time` and `keepalive_timeout`. |
| The check does not win in each incident | D wins in a partition and in a stall, and it ties C for a pod that cannot serve and for a failed prober. In the shared dependency, D had more failed requests than C in each mode and recovered later. |
| A stream timeout closes the whole session | One session carries each stream of its client. In a partition, each close failed approximately 50 streams, thus A and B had fewer failed requests than D. |

Mark asked for as much unification as possible. The check is already one with the gRPC client: the same PING, the same knob names and units, and the heartbeat of the core. Thus the unification is in the parts, and not in a default.

This evidence changes the decision:

1. The first caller of the fleet on HTTP/2. The package then gets a client of HTTP/2 with the check in it. Its knobs have no default, as the keepalive of the gRPC client has none. A new client is a MINOR of the service.
2. A cell where D does not lose to C in the shared dependency, for example with a stream timeout above the `request_timeout` of Redis.
3. A cell of long streams, where a quiet stream is normal. The bound is then 2 PINGs each second for each stream that waits, and the cell must give the cost of a close.

## How to read the tables

The column "Source" names the documents of the research. These documents are in jarvis.

- `SYSTEMS §13.9` is a section of the design of the program.
- `R3 O13` is a rule of the ordering law of R3, and `R5 §8 C7` is a ruling of R5.
- `H1 §2.8` is a section of the first digest of the earlier design, and `H2 log 5` is a part of the log of the second digest.

The column "Ev." gives the kind of evidence:

- **M**: a measurement of the program.
- **D**: a document, or arithmetic on documented values.
- **N**: a design note of Mark, and the measurements of a cluster in it.
- **R**: a ruling of the program.

"Disagreement" marks two sources that do not agree, and it tells which way the evidence goes. The column "In the service" names the part of `@mark1russell7/service` 0.4.1 that meets the requirement. It also names the item of [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md) that adds a missing part.
