# S6 · The requirements of the service abstraction

This file holds the requirements that `@mark1russell7/service` follows. They come from the first draft of S6, which jarvis keeps. That draft is of 2026-10-05, and it has 94 requirements. They come from the research rounds R1 to R5, and from two digests of an earlier health design by Mark.

This file moves the three groups that the runner follows: the lifecycle (A1 to A14), the health model (B1 to B19) and the configuration as data (E1 to E10). The other groups stay in the draft. Each group moves here with the part of the service that follows it. The status of S6 and its items are in [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md#s6--the-service-abstraction).

## The shape that the service follows

The draft proposes a shape and asks ten questions. Mark answered the first three on 2026-10-05, and Mark gave the other seven to Claude, decided as the draft recommends. The service follows these answers:

- **The declaration is the service.** A builder composes the service before it starts, with one method for each part. `describe()` gives the whole graph as data, and it starts nothing. Thus the declaration and the runtime cannot disagree.
- **A thin runtime.** The runtime takes each knob from the declaration, and it has no defaults of its own. A missing bound is infinite, not 0.
- **Readiness for each controller.** A controller declares its own dependencies. Thus a failed dependency fails only the routes that need it.
- **Two kinds of signal.** Active checks and the outcomes of live traffic are both evidence. A policy reads them and decides which one counts.
- **One timing shape.** Each policy is a debounce of polls or a window of time, and each one converts to the other. The algebra gets the detection bounds from either form.
- **A shutdown in declared phases.** Each owner adds its work to a phase with a cap, and the algebra checks the caps.
- **Machines as documents.** The machines of the runtime are `mark1russell7.machine/1` documents. The tests replay the transitions of the runtime against them.
- **Inequalities, not values.** The service states the inequalities that it needs from the platform. The scenario binds the values: the probes, the preStop delay and the grace period.
- **Wrappers, not patches.** A client is a wrapper of its library. A patch is only for a bug of the library, with a test that removes the patch after the fix.

## Unified or separate: the parts of a client wrapper

Thus each part of a client is in the core of the clients (`packages/service/src/client/`). A part goes into the entry of a driver only when a fact of that driver forces it. ### A call of the RabbitMQ client

A call of the RabbitMQ client is one of two things. It is a publish on a confirm channel until its confirm, or an RPC until its reply. The RPCs are `basic.get` and the passive declare of the round-trip check.

- An ack is `ok`. A nack is indeterminate, because a route with more than one queue can put the message on some of them.
- A reply code of the broker is a certain error. A call while the client is not ready, while it is blocked, or on a closed channel did not leave the client.
- A timeout, an abort, and the end of the channel or of the connection before the confirm or the reply are indeterminate.
- `ack` and `nack` of a delivery are frames with no reply, thus they are not calls. The end of their generation puts the message back on its queue: at least once.

The defaults come from the cells of R3, on amqplib 2.2.0 against RabbitMQ 4.3.6:

- `request_timeout` is 9.5 s. After a loss of the leader of a quorum queue, the first confirm came at 6.61 s, 6.68 s and 8.05 s (T11). The timeout also fits in the slowest poll of 10 s (O13).
- `heartbeat_interval` is 15 s. The heartbeat ended a silent connection at 45.009 s, before the kernel gave up at 53.016 s (O17). At the 60 s of the broker, it came at 180.009 s. A frame each 7.5 s keeps a flow through a router that forgets it at 30 s (O03).
- `connect_bound` is 5 s (DP44). `retry_base` is 1 s, because the cells connected to the next node 1 s after the end of a connection.

### A call of the HTTP client

A call of the HTTP client is one request until the end of the body of its response. The client keeps the whole body. The agent gives the socket back to its pool only at the end of the response. A body that ends early is not certain.

- A whole response is `ok`, with its status in the value. RFC 9110 does not say that a 5xx response did not apply, thus the client does not make it an error. The caller decides.
- A request leaves the client when it has a connected socket: a free socket that the agent reuses, or a new socket after its connect.
- Before that, the request did not leave the client. That is so in the queue of the agent, in a connect, at a refusal, at `connect_bound`, and at an abort or a close there.
- After that, a failure, a timeout and an abort are indeterminate. The client then destroys the socket, thus no later request gets a part of that response.

Idempotency comes from the method, by RFC 9110 §9.2.2. GET, HEAD, OPTIONS, TRACE, PUT and DELETE are idempotent, and POST and PATCH are not. The operation of a call is its method in upper case, or the `operation` that the call names. A spec can add a keyed operation, for example `POST /charge` with an `Idempotency-Key` header.

The keep-alive race is the reason for the second try. A server closes an idle connection at S. A request that goes on that socket between S − RTT and S gets a reset. Go sends a GET again on a new connection, and not a POST. The core gives the same rule: the client sends a GET again one time on a new socket, and not a POST.

The defaults come from the HTTP cells, on the agent of Node 24.21.0 against a Node server at its defaults:

- `pool_idle_expiry` is 4 s, and `hint_buffer` is 1 s. The server sends a hint of 5 s, and it closes an idle connection at 6 s (6.0003 s in the cells). The agent then closes a free socket at 4 s, below the close of the server (O01).
- `request_timeout` is 9.5 s, in the slowest poll of 10 s (O13). The agent has no timer of a call.
- `connect_bound` is 5 s (DP44). `keepalive_idle` is 1 s, the default of the agent.

A new client of S1 must use the core. It extends the shapes, it sends through `sendWithReissue` and `inFlight`, and it gives its checks through `stepChecks`. For each part that it keeps in its own entry, it adds a row to this table. The row gives the fact of the driver that forces the part. A part without such a fact moves into the core.

## How to read the tables

The column "Source" names the documents of the research. These documents are in jarvis.

- `SYSTEMS §13.9` is a section of the design of the program.
- `R3 O13` is a rule of the ordering law of R3, and `R5 §8 C7` is a ruling of R5.
- `H1 §2.8` is a section of the first digest of the earlier design, and `H2 log 5` is a part of the log of the second digest.

The column "Ev." gives the kind of evidence:

- **M**: a measurement of the program.
- **D**: a document, or arithmetic on documented values.
- **N**: a design note of Mark, and the measurements of a cluster in it.
- **R**: a ruling of the program.

"Disagreement" marks two sources that do not agree, and it tells which way the evidence goes. The column "In the service" names the part of `@mark1russell7/service` 0.4.1 that meets the requirement. It also names the item of [the roadmap](ROADMAP.md) that adds a missing part.

## B · The health model

| ID | Requirement | Source | Ev. | In the service |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | The process computes three verdicts. Started goes in one direction only. Ready is started, not in the drain, alive, and with no failed readiness sequence. Alive is no liveness window over its limit. Liveness passes from the start of the process. | H2 log 3–4, H1 §2.4 | N | `health.verdict()` gives started, draining, ready and alive. A liveness latch starts released. |
| B2 | Health is a chain of three state machines: the raw result, a gate for each check, and the counter of the kubelet with the propagation. The gate fails after 3 polls, recovers after 1, and starts tripped. The algebra computes each lag. | SYSTEMS §13.9, H1 §2.0 | N, R | A latch for each check that gates a policy. The declaration gives the counts. `failSpan` and `recoverSpan` give the lags. |
| B3 | Each check polls on its own loop. The probes answer from the cache in constant time, on a health port of their own. | H1 §2.2 | N | `health.probe(kind)` answers from the cache. SYS-2 gives a probe server on its own port, and a probe handler for an app port. |
| B4 | The outcome of a check is ok, error or indeterminate (nothing measured). Only ok passes. A timeout is an error, and a throw is an error with a flag. This outcome is not the same as the unknown outcome of an operation. | H1 §2.3, §6.1, R1 §1.1 | N, D | A check answers `"ok"`, `"error"` or `"indeterminate"`. An indeterminate poll moves no sequence. |
| B5 | Each poll makes a new measurement, because a memoized check once hid an outage for up to an hour. The runtime does not start a pending use again. Each later poll gives the same cause at once, and the sequence continues to count. | H1 §2.8, §2.12, P10, H2 log 5 | N | A pending use is not started again. Each later poll fails at once with the same cause. |
| B6 | The timeout of a check is long enough for a cold dial, and shorter than its interval: 4 s against 5 s (O12). The intervals have a jitter of ±20 %, on a schedule of fixed delay. | H1 §2.8–2.9, R3 O12 | N, D | Each check declares its interval, its jitter and its timeout. `lint()` applies O12. |
| B7 | Each step of a driver that a check exercises has a finite timeout, in one poll with its jitter. A check that hangs heals only when its driver heals: after 924.6 s in flight, and not at any time while idle. R3 finds O13 violated in 6 of 7. | SYSTEMS §13.9, H1 §3.5, R3 O13 | N, D | `lint()` applies O13. The Redis client binds its knobs for it. |
| B8 | The readiness latch fails after 3 and recovers after 1, and it starts tripped. A late registration takes the pod out until its first pass. A sequence or an interval of zero fails the decoding. | H1 §2.4, H2 log 1, R1 §6.5 | N, D | A readiness latch starts tripped, and so does a late check until its first pass. The builder refuses a zero interval or count. |
| B9 | Liveness windows count on the monotonic clock from the first failure of a sequence: 45 s for a wedge of the process, 5 min for a dependency as a last resort, and 0 for a check that times itself. Disagreement: H1 used the wall clock. H2 moved to the monotonic clock, because the real-time clock steps each 30 s in R4 and R5. | H1 §2.5, §2.9, H2 log 3, 7, R4 §9 | N, M | A window measures time on the monotonic clock from the first failure of a sequence. |
| B10 | A liveness failure fails readiness, and readiness detects before liveness (W8, O09). In kind without that coupling, each node refused 74–86 connections during a liveness restart. With the coupling, no node refused a connection. | SYSTEMS §13.9, H2 §2.5–2.6, R4 §5 | N, M | `couple({ from: "liveness", to: "readiness", effect: "fails" })`. No coupling applies without a declaration. `lint()` applies O09. |
| B11 | The model keeps apart the four ways that a pod stops: a deletion (preStop ≥ propagation, O04), a liveness restart (W8), a crash, and a stop for lack of memory (OOM). Disagreement: H2 expected a crashed pod to stay in its endpoints until the readiness threshold. R4 measured its removal in 0.28–0.33 s. | H2 §2.4, R4 §5, R3 O04 | N, M | The hold of `fail readiness` binds `readiness_hold@process`, and `lint()` applies O04. | B12 | Workers stay out of readiness. Readiness does not depend on other internal services, because Kubernetes has no panic floor. Liveness does not examine readiness. | H1 §2.6, §3.7, P10, R3 O14 | N, D | Each check names the policy that it gates. `lint()` applies O14. |
| B13 | The gating posture is data on the registration. The gating flag is necessary and has no default: the law of REQUIRED-nullable, which the notes found independently. | H1 §2.1, §6.4, H2 log 1 | N, R | Each check declares `gates`, with no default. |
| B14 | Each check names the step that it exercises: dial, local state, round trip, checkout, progress, topology or latency. It also tells if it uses the connection of the application. | B15 | The probe settings of the platform are declared knobs. Disagreement: H2 asks for a poll interval shorter than the probe period, but it probes each 2 s against a poll of 5 s. | B16 | Propagation is a declared distribution. For each node, it is p50 1.1 s, p99 8 s and p99.9 24 s. After a preStop of 20 s, the platform still routes approximately 28 % of deletions. Disagreement: H2 gives 15 s, and R3 binds the law at p99.
