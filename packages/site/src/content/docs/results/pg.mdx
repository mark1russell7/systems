These are the results of the PostgreSQL cells, from 2026-10-18. The client is node-postgres: pg 8.23.1 with pg-pool 3.14.0 on Node 24.21.0, as one `pg.Pool` at its defaults. The server is PostgreSQL 17.11. T01 is the direct pair.

The machine documents `node-postgres` and `postgres-session` give each band, and the algebra composes the pool over Linux TCP. At the defaults, pg sets no keepalive, and it has no timer for a query or for a connect. Thus the idle timer of the pool and the kernel are the only detectors.

CI started the cells at main in the run [39104794591](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/39104794591), on 6 hosted runners of GitHub. The kernel is Linux 6.17, with HZ 1000. Each cell has 11 runs in 2 fresh environments, with one warm-up run in each environment. A `fin` stops the server, thus each of its runs has its own environment.

Each of the 13 cells holds against the prediction of the algebra. The cells have 143 counted runs, and no run is void.

## What the cells found

- **The idle timer of the pool closes an idle client at 10 s.** With no fault, the pool closes it 10.004 s after its last segment. Behind a black hole, the close comes at 10.003 s too.
  - Behind a black hole, the pool emits `remove` only when the socket closes, and the FIN of the pool gets no answer. Thus the kernel stamps the close: the change from ESTABLISHED to FIN_WAIT1.
- **No timer of pg ends a query at the defaults.** A server that holds each reply 5 s (`pg_sleep`) gives the reply at 5.006 s, and no failure comes. A frozen server has no detector in 60 s. Only the kernel ends a query in a black hole, at 924.6 s at its defaults.
- **The timers of the pool place the borders of O13 and O15.**
  - `query_timeout` ends a query in a black hole at 9.503 s and 10.502 s, on each side of the slowest poll of 10 s.
  - `connectionTimeoutMillis` ends the first connect at 29.007 s and 31.009 s, on each side of the startup budget of 30 s. The kernel gives up on the SYNs only at 131 s.
- **A band of a fault before the first connect counts from that connect.** The pool arms its connect timer at the first query, 3.7 ms after the first line of the client. Since harness 0.11.3, the client writes a `connecting` line at its first query, and the band counts from it. Before that line, the two cells of O15 ended 1 to 2 ms after their bands.
- **A reset or a FIN comes at once.** A reset of a query in flight gives ECONNRESET in 0.777 ms, and a reset of the SYN refuses the first connect in 4.779 ms. The FIN of a stopped server ends an idle client in 17 µs.

The plan of R3 has 23 cells of T01 over these two systems. Ten of them wait, and the catalog gives a reason for each:

- Three need a fault that the harness cannot make: a restart at the same address, a fault at a reply, and a blocked event loop.
- Six are borders. They need a router between the client and the server, a client that the application holds, or a slow query only in the second query.
- pg sets TCP_NODELAY, thus the pattern of write-write-read does not occur.
