These are the results of the HTTP cells, from 2026-10-17. The server is the `http.Server` of Node 24.21.0, as a script on the stock Node image. The clients are the `http.Agent` of Node 24.21.0, an HTTP/1.1 `Client` of undici 8.12.2 and the `http.Transport` of Go 1.27.2. The cells of T01 are a direct pair. The cells of T03 put a NAT router between the client and the server.

CI started the cells on the hosted runners of GitHub. The kernel is Linux 6.17, with HZ 1000, and the coarse clock of the kernel has a step of 1 ms. Each cell has 11 runs in 2 fresh environments, with one warm-up run in each environment. The full run of the catalogs at main, [39074841434](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/39074841434), did the 39 cells with each other catalog, in 12 shards.

Of the 39 cells, 38 hold against the prediction of the algebra, and 1 is a measurement only. No run of the 429 is void.

The first run of these cells, on 2026-10-16, found one gap. The pool of a Node client starts its idle timer some milliseconds after the last segment of the reply. The model of the cells had no such delay, thus 4 cells failed. A calibration cell of each pool measures that time of handling, and each band includes it.

## The keep-alive race

Two requests go on one connection, with a gap g between the first reply and the second request. The server closes an idle connection at S after its response: `keepAliveTimeout` plus `keepAliveTimeoutBuffer`, 6 s at the defaults. The pool of the client drops the connection at C after the reply. The one-way delays cancel, except for the round trip.

| Ordering | Condition | What the second request meets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `kept` | g + RTT < S, and g < C | the same connection, and a reply |
| `dropped` | C < g, and C < S | a new connection: the pool closed the old one |
| `fin-first` | S < g, and S < C | a new connection: the FIN came before the send |
| `crossed` | S − RTT < g < S, and g < C | the FIN of the server in flight, and a reset from its kernel |

The agent and undici take C from the hint of the server, less a buffer. The hint is S in whole seconds or less, thus their C stays below S. Go ignores the hint, and its C is 90 s. Thus only Go races at the defaults.

## What the cells found

- **Go races as the model gives.** A GET 3 s after the reply goes on the same connection. A GET 6.5 s after the reply meets a connection that the server closed at 6.0003 s, thus it goes on a new connection. At a gap of 6 s, the gap meets the close of the server in its window. Then 9 runs used a new connection, and 2 sent the GET again.
- **A crossing needs a long round trip.** With 100 ms of latency, a GET 5.95 s after the reply meets the FIN of the server in flight. Go sends the GET again on a new connection, and it gets its reply. A POST gets the end of file, because Go does not send a POST again.
- **The borders of O01 hold for each client.** A GET 50 ms before C goes on the same connection, and a GET 50 ms after C goes on a new one. Each pool closes its connection before the server does, thus no request meets a FIN. The plan gives O01 a margin of 0.26 s at S = 6 s. The documents give a margin of a few milliseconds, and the cells agree with the documents.
- **The pools of Node close a few milliseconds after the reply, in their bands.** The agent and undici start the idle timer after they handle the reply. Each band includes the time of handling that the calibration of the pool measured:
  - the agent at `keepAliveTimeout 1500`, whose hint of 1 s gives C = 0, closes at 2.5 ms. Its band ends at 7.73 ms.
  - undici at `keepAliveTimeout 2500`, whose hint of 2 s gives C = 0, closes at 5.62 ms. Its band ends at 12.18 ms.
  - undici between C (3 s) and S (6 s) closes at 3.0078 s, and its band ends at 3.0172 s.
  - undici at `keepAliveTimeoutThreshold 100` closes at 5.9126 s, and its band ends at 5.9201 s.

  In the first run, these 4 cells failed by 3 ms to 8 ms, because their bands had no time of handling.
- **Go sends a GET again after a failure on a reused connection.** Its new try then meets the fault. A reset ends the request at 234 µs with `ECONNRESET`, and the new try gets `ECONNREFUSED`. An unreachable host ends the request at 3.12 s with `EHOSTUNREACH`, and the new try gets `EHOSTUNREACH` too. Behind a black hole at the router, the retransmissions give up at 6.16 s, and the dial of the new try times out at `DialTimeout`.
- **A timer of the client comes before the keepalive of the kernel.** Behind a black hole at the router, an idle Go connection closes at its `IdleConnTimeout`, 9.007 s with the timers of Go scaled by 10. Thus the client gets no error. A frozen server gives undici `UND_ERR_HEADERS_TIMEOUT` at 10.187 s, from its `headersTimeout`.
- **Go waits for a held reply.** The server holds a reply for 60 s, and nothing ends the request of Go in a window of 20 s. At its defaults, Go has no timeout for a reply.
- **No run is void.** In the first run, 7 runs were void. In each, the retransmit timer was 3 to 8 jiffies behind its wheel slot, thus the VM delayed it, not the kernel. The page of [the timers behind their slot](../timers/) tells how the prober finds such a delay.

## Where the data comes from

Each runner wrote a raw record of each of its cells. A record has the cell and the manifests of its environments. It also has each warm-up run, and each run with its kernel records and the lines of the client and the server.

The script `packages/site/scripts/http.ts` reads each record with the code of the harness, and it stops when a verdict disagrees with the summary of the run. It also stops when a prediction disagrees with the catalog at HZ 1000. It writes the copy that this page shows, and that copy names no machine. The cells and their predictions are in the [catalogs](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/tree/main/packages/cells).
