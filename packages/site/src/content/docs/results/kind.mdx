These are the results of the T07 and T18 cells, from 2026-10-18. Each cell operates in kind: Kubernetes 1.37.0 in Docker, with one control plane and two workers. The server is the service of this repository, `@mark1russell7/service`, as a Deployment behind a Service. A client on each node sends a request each 20 ms, each on a new connection to the ClusterIP.

T07 is the endpoint lifecycle of a Service. Its question is O04 with the hold of the service. The listener of a leaving pod stays open for its preStop sleep and its hold of `fail readiness`. A connection fails when the rules of its node select the pod after that. T18 is a fleet of 3 replicas, and the readiness of each replica needs Redis 7.4.

CI started the cells at main in the run [39090689204](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/39090689204), on 10 hosted runners of GitHub. The kernel is Linux 6.17, with HZ 1000, and the three modes of kube-proxy operate on it. The cells of one mode get new clusters in sequence. Each cluster does its share of the 11 runs of each cell, after one warm-up run.

Of the 31 cells, 26 hold against the prediction, and no cell fails. 3 cells are the calibrations of the propagation, and 2 are measurements only. No run of the 341 is void. The calibration changes the windows of the catalog, thus a script judges each record again with the catalog after the calibration.

The client waits 2 s, thus the retransmission of a lost SYN gets through. A pause cell lets a stale SYN_SENT entry fail its node up to 122 s before the stop of the intake. Two changes after the run of 2026-10-17 removed its 4 failures:

- **The lab forgets the old handshakes.** Before each run, the lab deletes each conntrack entry in SYN_SENT to the ClusterIP. The clients send requests between the runs, and such an entry kept its NAT for 120 s. Thus a client that took its port again in the next run timed out.
- **The prediction has the CLOSE entry of a reset** (kind 0.1.6). A reset leaves its flow in CLOSE for 10 s, with the NAT to its pod. A later connection from the same port goes to that pod.

## What the cells found

- **No lone failure is left.** On 2026-10-17, each of the 4 cells that failed lost one or two lone requests in 11 runs. In this run, each failed request is in the prediction of its cell.
- **A reset leaves a CLOSE entry that a reused port meets.** With no hold and no preStop on `iptables`, the pod resets a connection in flight. Conntrack keeps its flow in CLOSE for 10 s, with the NAT to the pod.
  - A SYN on a CLOSE entry looks up again only after a FIN, or after a reset from the client. Thus a later connection from the same port goes to the deleted pod, and it times out.
  - In one run, a client used the port of a reset again 8.2 s later, and no counter of its node grew. The run on the branch of #50 had the same case, 6.8 s after its reset.
  - The prediction has this mechanism in `iptables` and `nftables`, thus the cell holds. IPVS keeps its own table of connections, and no run of it had the case.
- **The propagation of a deletion depends on the mode of kube-proxy.** The calibration measures the time from the deletion of a pod to the first poll of the rules of each node without the pod. The median is 51 ms for `nftables`, 53 ms for `iptables` and 128 ms for `ipvs`. The slowest of 33 times is 131 ms, 92 ms and 241 ms.
- **O04 with the hold of the service decides a deletion, in each mode.** With the hold of 2 s of the design, no request failed in the 33 runs. With no hold and no preStop, O04 does not hold. Then the failed requests start between the stop of the intake and the propagation. The median run lost 2 requests on `nftables` and on `iptables`, and 13 on `ipvs`, which propagates more slowly.
- **A pause of kube-proxy opens the window on its node.** kube-proxy on one node stops for 5 s, from 0.5 s before a deletion. With the hold of 2 s, the clients of the paused node fail until that node syncs: 57 to 58 failed requests in the median run. With a hold of 8 s, the pod stays open longer than the pause, and the prediction is that no request fails. In each mode, no request failed.
- **A stale conntrack entry catches a later connection.** The paused node sends each new connection to the address of the gone pod. The SYN of each connection makes a conntrack entry in SYN_SENT, and that entry stays for 120 s. A later connection from the same source port meets the entry, and it goes to the address of the pod again. The windows of the pause cells include that time, thus the late failures of the run of 2026-10-16 are in the prediction.
- **A rolling update loses no request.** The rules wait `minSyncPeriod` after a sync before they drop the old pod. The hold of 2 s covers that wait, and no request failed in 11 runs.
- **A liveness that fails readiness keeps each connection.** When liveness fails readiness, the pod leaves the Service before the kubelet stops its container. No connection failed, and the service answered 503 for that time. Without that coupling, the endpoint stays until the container stops, and the median run had 22 failed requests.
- **T18: a readiness that needs Redis empties the Service in its band.** After a reset, a freeze, a black hole or a loss of Redis, the Service had no ready endpoint 33 s to 43.7 s after the fault. The band is the latch, then 3 probes of the kubelet 10 s apart, then the propagation. The times are near 33 s or near 43 s, one period of the probe apart.
- **A readiness that does not need Redis keeps the fleet in the Service.** Behind a black hole to Redis, no replica leaves the Service, and no request is refused. The requests that need Redis time out instead: 8,574 in the median run.
- **Liveness and the stall are in their bands.** With readiness coupled into liveness, a black hole to Redis restarts each replica. SIGTERM comes at 39.225 s in the median run, in the band of 28 s to 50 s. Without the coupling, no replica got SIGTERM. A blocked event loop removes its replica at 29.07 s, in the band of 21 s to 31.105 s.
- **A rollout waits for Redis.** While Redis has no endpoint, no old pod goes away and no new pod becomes ready. In the median run, the first new pod is ready 6.471 s after Redis is back.
- **Two cells are measurements only.** A crash of the server process gives 21 failed requests in the median run. The kubelet finds the exit at its own relist, and no document gives that time. A throttle of `cpu.max` at 1 ms in each 1 s gives 1,282 failed requests in the median run.

## S2: how callers learn that a pod cannot serve

One replica of 2 cannot serve for 30 s. A flag file fails its route with 503, and the same flag fails its readiness check. The clients keep 8 keep-alive connections each, and kube-proxy selects a backend only for a new connection. The S2 cells come from the run [40358038728](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/40358038728), with 3 counted runs of each cell in each mode. The cells of D come from the run [40394320219](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/40394320219). The judge counted 3 runs of each cell of D in each mode.

- **A: endpoints only.** The edge keeps each connection open. The removal of the endpoint changes nothing for an open connection, thus its requests fail until the heal.
- **B: in band (lame duck).** While the service is not ready, each answer has `Connection: close`. Each caller then opens a new connection, and the rules send it to the other replica after the removal.
- **C: in band on HTTP/2 (GOAWAY).** The same lame duck on an edge of HTTP/2 without TLS. While the service is not ready, the edge sends GOAWAY on each session. Each client sends its requests as streams on one session, thus it opens a new connection through the rules after the GOAWAY.
- **D: in band on HTTP/2 with a session check.** C, and each client checks its session. A stream timeout closes its session. After 0.5 s with no frame from the peer, the client sends a PING, and with no ACK in 0.5 s, it closes the session.

A and B differ only in the field `lameDuck` of the declaration. C is B with the field `protocol` set to `h2c`, and one session for each client. D is C with the field `sessionCheck` of the workload. The mode of kube-proxy gives the propagation of the removal, thus the end of the window of the lame duck. The table gives the verdict, the failed requests of the median run, and the prediction of each candidate.

Each connection on the target fails one request in each turn of 160 ms. Thus the count of failed requests follows the connections on the target at the fault. IPVS selects the backends in turn, thus 12 of the 24 connections go to the target. The rules of iptables and nftables select at random, thus the count is binomial. It is 7 to 17 in 95 % of the runs at the least. The runs had 8 to 16.

C has fewer connections, thus each connection carries more requests. Each session on the target fails one request in each turn of 20 ms, thus its window ends 140 ms earlier than B at the latest. The 3 sessions give the same mean count as the 24 connections of B, but a wider band. From 0 to 3 sessions go to the target, and a run with none has no failure.

In the random modes, the mean of C is about that of B: 983 against 930 failed requests. But C spreads wider, 0 to 1,893 against 540 to 1,128, and its medians in iptables, ipvs and nftables are 1,087, 2,133 and 706. IPVS put all 3 sessions on the target in each run, and one run of iptables had none and no failure.

The target answers each stream of D at once, thus the session check closed no session. Thus D has the prediction of C, and each counted run of D is in it. The medians of D in iptables, ipvs and nftables were 580, 1,608 and 572 failed requests. The count follows the sessions on the target and the end of the window. One run of nftables had no session on the target, and no failure.

### How callers learn

S6 scores each candidate of health and routing on seven criteria, for each incident of its design. Nine runs score eight incidents. They are a pod that cannot serve, a partitioned pod, a stalled pod and a failed prober. A black-holed shared dependency, a flapping dependency, a lost zone and a slow dependency are the other four. The third run adds D to the first two incidents. Each of the next six incidents has its own run, with each candidate.

The table puts the measure of each criterion next to its prediction, and each cell links to its run. A criterion that the run cannot support has no number, only the reason.

B, C and D each have fewer failed requests than A in each mode, by the median run. B has the fewest in IPVS, and D has the fewest in iptables and nftables. D has the band of C, thus the two are equal for a pod that cannot serve. Thus the service keeps one rule: while it is not ready, it closes each connection on the channel of its protocol. HTTP/1.1 gets `Connection: close`, and HTTP/2 gets GOAWAY. A is an opt-in of the transport.

In a partition, the service is ready, thus no candidate gets a signal in band. Each caller learns from its own timeouts. A and B learned in 1.4 s at the most, and C in approximately 26 s, after the heal. D learned in 1.07 s to 1.46 s by the median run, from the PING of its session check. Thus the rule stays, and the client of HTTP/2 also checks its session (D). The scores of the partition measure the time until callers know from the fault.

A stall gives the same result. The service stays ready, and A and B learned in 1.15 s at the most. C learned only at the resume, approximately 10 s after the fault. D learned in 1.28 s to 3.3 s by the median run. Thus the stall changes no part of the rule.

A failed prober does not change the rule either. Each candidate removes the target falsely, because the kubelet reads the same readiness check. A failed 1,529 to 1,690 requests by the median run, and B, C and D failed 3 to 25. B, C and D learned 10.4 s to 16.23 s after the fault by the median run, and A only after the heal. The scores of the failed prober also measure the time of the false removal.

A black-holed shared dependency keeps the rule too, and the readiness of the service follows its dependency. The service cannot tell a shared failure from a failure of its own replica. For a failure of one replica, the removal routes callers around that replica. In a shared failure no replica can serve, thus the removal costs no requests. B had the fewest failed requests in each mode, and it recovered first.

The cost is the time until callers know. The callers of A got a fast 503 after approximately 12 s, and B, C and D failed slowly until the heal. A gate that keeps the endpoints in a shared failure was a separate decision with its own evidence. The shared gate (E) gave B the fast 503s of A, but it did not cut the failed requests. Thus the service does not get it. The scores of the shared dependency measure when the Service is empty, the last slow failure and the last failure after the heal.

A flapping dependency changes no part of the rule. Each fault of the run gave the result of one shared failure, and the readiness of the service followed each fault and each heal. B had the fewest failed requests in each mode again, and it recovered first. The scores of the flapping dependency have one value for each fault of each counted run, thus 9 values for each cell.

A lost zone changes no part of the rule. The frozen service sends no signal, thus the rule gives none. A and B learned from their timeouts in 1.06 s at the most. The node lifecycle controller removed the target 45.4 s to 50.7 s after the fault by the median run. C failed until the 9th retransmission, after the heal, and D bounded it by its session check. The scores of the lost zone measure the removal of the target and the time until it is ready again after the heal.

## Where the data comes from

Each runner wrote a raw record of each of its cells. A record has the cell and the manifests of its clusters. It also has each run with the lines of the clients, of the watcher and of the agent of each node.

The script `packages/site/scripts/kind.ts` judges each record with the cell of the same name in the catalog. It stops when a verdict disagrees with the summary of the run. It writes the copy that this page shows, and that copy names no machine. The cells and their predictions are in the [catalogs](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/tree/main/packages/cells).
