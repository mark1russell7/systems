These are the results of step 3 of the testing program, from 2026-10-17. In step 3, the harness puts a chain of hops between the client and a stock Redis 7.4. A hop is a router or a proxy. Three new catalogs use the chain: T03 through a NAT router, T02 through HAProxy, and TOX with Toxiproxy as the subject. Each cell has 11 runs in 2 fresh environments, and each environment starts with one warm-up run that the verdict does not count.

CI started the cells on the hosted runners of GitHub. The kernel is Linux 6.17, with HZ 1000. The full run of the catalogs at main, [39074841434](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/actions/runs/39074841434), did the 46 cells with each other catalog, in 12 shards.

Of the 46 cells, 39 hold against the prediction of the algebra, and 7 are measurements only. Each of these 7 cells measures what HAProxy or Toxiproxy does. The program has no machine document for these two proxies yet, thus the algebra cannot predict them. No cell fails, and no run of the 506 is void.

The first run of step 3, on 2026-10-16, was on a development VM with Linux 6.6 at HZ 250. It had 42 cells, and 5 of them failed. The section [what the first run found](#what-the-first-run-found) keeps what its runs showed.

## What the cells found

- **O03 decides all three forms.** A keepalive at 4 s keeps the flow in a router that forgets at 5 s, thus nothing detects anything in 15 s. At 6 s, the router forgets the flow 4.9998 s to 5.051 s after the last segment. Then a `reset-both` router resets the flow at the forget, with a median of 5.039 s. A `reset-next` router resets the first probe (6.305 s), and a `silent-drop` router lets the keepalive give up (9.322 s). These are the times of the algebra.
- **The server side obeys the same border.** At `tcp-keepalive 6`, a `reset-both` router resets the two ends at 5.027 s. A `reset-next` router drops the probe of Redis, and Redis gets `ETIMEDOUT` at 12.44 s. Under `none`, the stack of the router resets the probe at 6.24 s. After 8 s idle, a request under `recreate` starts the flow again, and each run got its reply.
- **A fault at the router gives the time of the direct pair.** The router puts each fault on its device toward one side. The client meets what the rules of the fault let through, as on the direct pair. A host unreachable from the router ends a request at 3.12 s, and a black hole at 6.176 s. An idle connection behind a black hole ends at 11.34 s. A reset from the router ends a request at 122 µs. Each cell has the band of the same fault on [the network failures](../faults/).
- **The conntrack of a router still acts behind its own black hole.** Conntrack sees each segment before the rules of the fault drop it. Thus each retransmission of the client refreshes the flow. The router forgets the flow 5 s after the last retransmission before a longer wait. At `tcp_retries2 = 8`, a `reset-both` router then resets the two ends at 11.602 s, before the give-up of the client. Idle, a `reset-both` router resets at 5.035 s. A plain NAT router lets a late probe into the black hole, thus the keepalive gives up at 9.399 s.
- **The idle timeouts of HAProxy ignore TCP keepalive.** With a client keepalive of 2 s, `timeout client 5s` closes the connection at 5.003 s. With a probe from Redis each 2 s, `timeout server 5s` also closes at 5.003 s. Thus a keepalive that satisfies O03 for a middlebox at layer 4 does nothing for a proxy at layer 7. O03 has no clause for the idle timer of a proxy yet.
- **The client learns of a dead server only through HAProxy.** A black hole at Redis reaches the client as an EOF at 6.112 s. That is when the keepalive of HAProxy gave up on Redis: its socket gave up at 6.105 s. On the leg of the client, the socket of HAProxy gave up at 6.111 s, and the keepalive of the client took 11.318 s.
- **Toxiproxy changes resets and timeouts into normal closes.** A reset at the server reaches the client through Toxiproxy as an EOF at 0.437 ms. On the direct pair, the same reset is `ECONNRESET` at 0.096 ms, as the algebra predicts. A black hole at the server reaches the client as an EOF at 6.573 s. That is the give-up of the retransmissions of Toxiproxy itself, at `tcp_retries2 = 3` in its namespace. Thus a client behind Toxiproxy gets no reset and no timeout from the far end.
- **`reset_peer` on a live connection sends a FIN, not a reset.** The documentation says "Simulate TCP RESET". But in the source, the close with linger 0 applies only to a connection that starts after the toxic. The catalog predicts the FIN from the source, and each of the 11 runs ended with EOF in its band, with a median of 512 µs.
- **Each band through Toxiproxy includes its own time.** Its documentation gives no time of its own. Thus a calibration cell measured it: a 99th percentile of 0.38 ms toward Redis and 0.35 ms toward the client. Each band adds that time for each crossing. Thus `limit_data` closes in its band, at a median of 0.913 ms. The 51 slices of `slicer`, 10 ms apart, take 641.6 ms: approximately 2.6 ms more for each slice than its delay. The toxic `bandwidth` at 100 KB/s moves 500,011 bytes in 5.014 s.
- **No run is void, and each warm-up is in its band.** The runners shrink the hash table of conntrack to 16384 buckets. Thus a listing of the table is short, and the longest time of a segment through the router was 0.315 ms.

## T03: through a NAT router

<Chain catalog="T03" client:visible />

The router is the router image of the harness: a NAT with conntrack. It forgets an established flow when the flow is idle for 5 s, the 350 s of a cloud NAT divided by 70. Its form tells what a late packet meets after the forget. The prediction of each cell comes from the machine documents of conntrack and of Linux TCP. The law O03 decides if the forget occurs at all: each idle flow must get a refresh before each middlebox on its path forgets it.

On a chart of T03, a diamond on the bottom row is the time when the router forgot the flow. It counts from the last segment that the client received, as each detection of an idle cell does.

### DV-12 on the two sides of O03

DV-12 is the case of a broken O03: the client holds a connection that the router forgot. Its window is the time from the forget to the detection. The table puts a keepalive of the client on each side of the border of O03, for each of the three forms. The router forgets at 5 s.

## T02: through HAProxy

<Chain catalog="T02" client:visible />

HAProxy 3.2.25 runs in TCP mode between the client and Redis. It ends each connection of the client and opens its own connection to Redis, thus each leg has its own sockets and its own timers. Each socket of HAProxy has kernel keepalive (`option clitcpka` and `option srvtcpka`), at 3 s idle and 3 probes 1 s apart. Its namespace sets `tcp_retries2 = 3`.

## What the first run found

The first run of step 3 was on a development VM with Linux 6.6 at HZ 250, on 2026-10-16. Another job loaded the host for all of that time. The harness did the cells of T03 one at a time, because the listing of a router reads the conntrack table of the whole kernel. Of its 42 cells, 29 held, 8 were measurements only, and 5 failed:

- **Two cells were findings about Toxiproxy.** `reset_peer` ended each run with EOF, where the catalog predicted a reset. `limit_data` closed 1.307 ms to 4.777 ms after the request, where its band had no time of Toxiproxy. The catalog has both of these causes.
- **Three cells failed on one outlier run each.** The prober explained each outlier, and no outlier came back on a re-run.
  - The T01 stall: the first round trip of one run took 4.25 ms, where the others took 0.14 ms to 1.3 ms. Thus its RTO was 52 jiffies, not 51, and the reply came back at 1.736 s, after the band that ended at 1.709 s.
  - The T03 cell `recreate`: one reply took 11.5 ms. The request reached Redis at 1.4 ms, and no side sent a retransmission. Thus 10 ms passed in Redis or in the forward path of the router, and no record tells which.
  - The TOX cell `timeout 2000`: in one run, the client got the close at 2.017 s. The state record of the prober, in the namespace of Toxiproxy, showed that Toxiproxy closed its own socket 15.7 ms late.
- **Three warm-up runs landed outside their bands.** Each was the first toxic of an environment, and one had a first API commit of 16.9 ms. The verdict does not count a warm-up.
- **Ten runs were void.**
  - A slow listing of the router voided 5 runs. The router lists its table each 50 ms, and a `reset-both` router sends its resets after the listing that finds the expired flow. 3,201 listings took longer than the bound of 20 ms (`LISTING`), and the worst took 100.5 ms.
  - A commit of a toxic through the API of Toxiproxy took 13 ms to 17 ms on the first commits of an environment. That voided 1 run, because its commit was longer than δ<sub>abs</sub>, 4 ms.
  - In 4 runs, a timer of the kernel was two jiffies or more behind its wheel slot, thus the VM delayed it. The page of [the timers behind their slot](../timers/) tells how the prober finds such a delay.
- **The T01 slice held.** 10 cells of T01 checked that the generalized lab gives a direct pair as before. Each T01 cell runs on that lab, thus [the network failures](../faults/) give each of them.

## Where the data comes from

The runner of the cells wrote a raw record of each cell. A record has the cell, the manifests of its environments, each warm-up run and each run with its kernel records. The final measure of the harness then judged each record again. The script `packages/site/scripts/topologies.ts` reads each record with the code of the harness, and it stops when a verdict disagrees. It writes the copy that this page shows, and that copy names no machine. The cells and their predictions are in the [catalogs](https://github.com/mark1russell7/systems/tree/main/packages/cells).
