The harness must know its own noise before it can tell a finding from noise. Stage 0 measures that noise with two reference cells. Each cell has 30 runs at 1, 4 and 8 environments at the same time, and each environment has its own prober.

- **A reset on the next send.** The target answers the next segment of the client with a reset. No timer is in the path. Thus the time from the send to the error is the noise of the pipeline.
- **A scaled keepalive.** This cell is the same as Q1. Its spread at each level tells if more environments change the timing of a timer.

<Calibration client:visible />

## What the calibration sets

- **δ_abs is 4 ms.** δ_abs is the 99.9th percentile of the latency of a reset, at the concurrency cap or less. Its minimum is one jiffy, which is 4 ms at HZ 250. Each reset was shorter than that minimum.
- **The concurrency cap is 4.** In an earlier run at 8 environments, one reset waited 18.6 ms for the scheduler. Up to 4 environments, each reset took less than 2 ms.
- **More environments do not change the spread of keepalive.** At 1, 4 and 8 environments, the runs stay in the same band.

The rule for the cap reads the 99th percentile of 30 runs, which is the worst run. Thus one slow wake can set the cap to 1. A better rule, with more runs for each level or a lower percentile, is an open question.

Stage 0 occurs again after each change to the harness, the kernel or Docker.
